\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

This study investigates capability scaling-down laws for LLM compression through controlled measurements, predictive relations, and independent validation. Compact pruning predictors reduce development configuration measurements within a controlled domain, while quantization benefits from configuration-based interpolation and medians. Distillation exhibits reuse penalties that vary with student size and evaluation distribution, motivating empirical data-requirement intervals and bounds. Prospective selection tests and a pristine-student control identify additional QA opportunities under nominal storage constraints. Together, these findings connect predictive accuracy, measurement requirements, and decision value, and clarify where capability-conditioned prediction is useful.
