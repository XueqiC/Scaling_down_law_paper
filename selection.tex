\section{Compression Method Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

We evaluate the decision value of the empirical prediction framework under nominal storage constraints. The selection rule compares feasible candidates using estimates appropriate to each prediction setting. Independent evaluation identifies substantial cross-method opportunity for question-answering loss, while a retrospective ablation shows that a development-derived method-priority policy attains the same question-answering regret as the final rule; numeric predictions provide a smaller additional benefit for mathematics. These results distinguish settings in which stable method preferences suffice from those in which configuration-level prediction improves selection. The measurement-efficiency evidence of Section~\ref{sec:efficiency} rests on a separate experiment, and the selection results test no form that enters no selection.

\subsection{Selection under Storage Constraints}
\label{sec:selection_rule}

Given a reference model, a capability and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ whose nominal storage, counted in stored matrix parameters, falls within $B$. The final rule calls
the pruning and quantization relations of Section~\ref{sec:laws} on a seen state and their
source-free medians on a new one; for distillation it calls a regression on the student state for mathematics and pre-specified constants otherwise (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:locked_rule}). A workload needing several capabilities at once replaces $\widehat L_{m,c}$ by the largest loss increase
$\max_c[\widehat L_c(M)-L_c(M_0)]$. On the controlled panel the choice selects the measured best method in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells, and on four fresh Pythia states it comes within 0.14 nats of the best candidate (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_regret}).

Each reference carries twelve candidates, four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the dense model and one student distilled in this round, at seventeen storage budgets (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). The opportunity at a budget is the loss of the best feasible quantization
candidate minus that of the best feasible candidate of any method; a policy's regret is the loss of
its choice minus that same candidate. On the 64 cells at which both policies find a feasible candidate, the rule selects the new student in 63. Its regret there is 0.000 nats on three references and 0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to
1.84 nats for a policy restricted to quantization. For mathematics, code and the largest loss increase the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats and the rule matches quantization to within 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the criterion pre-specified for this round. On 384 fresh question-answering items scored after the round, the rule's choice has a lower loss than the quantization-only choice by 0.45 to 1.82 nats on three references and is level on the fourth; a generation readout puts the same students ahead of that choice on exact match on three references and behind on one, and no better than their initial students, so the loss gain does not convert reliably into generation accuracy, and which endpoint the selector optimises decides which model it recommends (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:p1-behaviour}). The quantization-only regret decomposes into selection error inside quantization and cross-method opportunity: on Pythia 410M its 0.59 nats are 0.47 and 0.12, and the rule's choice is 0.09 nats better than the best quantization candidate. Table~\ref{tab:s3-ablation-body} compares policies on the same candidates: on question answering a development-derived method priority with no numeric prediction, the development medians and the final rule reach the same regret, 0.007 nats against 0.853 for quantization only, so the benefit rests on a stable difference between methods rather than on fine loss margins; on mathematics the medians are 0.012 nats better than the priority alone, and the source-conditioned relations improve on neither.
