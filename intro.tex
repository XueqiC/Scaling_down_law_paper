\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed language-model agents are specialized systems
\citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,jimenez2024swebench,liu2024agentbench} whose cost scales
with each token. The model that serves such a workload is usually a reference model reduced by
pruning, quantization, or distillation, and choosing the method and its strength currently requires
measuring each candidate. Compression does not reduce capabilities uniformly: across twelve public models the same pruning
density leaves QA the least damaged capability in some series and the most damaged in others
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}), and the ordering changes with model size and model family.

Existing compression laws predict an aggregate loss from sparsity, precision, training format, the
pre-pruning loss or the teacher loss
\citep{frantar2023scaling,kumar2024scaling,panferov2025unified,zhang2024scaling,busbridge2025distillation},
and studies of uneven damage document it for single tasks or single quantizers
\citep{jaiswal2023compressing,liu2025quantization,sengupta2025compression}. Three limitations
remain: the endpoint is aggregate, so a capability-specific response shape stays invisible; the
fits mix model states that differ in size and pretraining stage, so it is unclear which inputs a
relation may use when the state is new; and coefficients are rarely frozen before the test measurements.

We ask when the loss of a capability after compression can be predicted from the initial model
state and the configuration alone. The endpoint is the conditional loss on a fixed probe set
relative to a reference measured beforehand (Eq.~\ref{eq:interface}); the admitted inputs are the
initial state, meaning parameter count, pretraining tokens and reference capability losses, and
the intervention setting; no measurement of the compressed target is used. The study then proceeds in one
sequence: controlled measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and stage vary independently,
and on Gemma students under one distillation protocol, establish what a law must reproduce
(\S\ref{sec:observed}); an analysis of shape, conditions and mechanism explains why
(\S\ref{sec:why}); method-specific relations are built on the verified findings
(\S\ref{sec:laws}), tested with coefficients and predictions frozen in advance
(\S\ref{sec:general}), and carried into a selection rule confirmed prospectively
(\S\ref{sec:selection}). Each relation keeps the status assigned before its outcome was measured.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A systematic, capability-conditioned science of scaling down.} We replace the aggregate
loss of existing compression laws with the loss of each capability, measure pruning, quantization
and distillation under one endpoint and one validation standard, and hold every relation to
coefficients and predictions frozen before measurement, so that every claim carries the range on which it was tested and no more.
\item \textbf{A general account of what a scaling-down law may share and what it may use.} Response
shapes, and not only response sizes, differ across capabilities, so a law may share structure only
where shapes agree; and interpolating within a measured model differs from transferring to a new
model state in the inputs it admits, so source information must be validated separately from
configuration information. Both principles decide the form of any such law beyond the panels
studied here, and the compact relation they admit earns its place by measurement efficiency:
fitted on half of the development configuration measurements, it matches the full-budget
regression on new states in a registered confirmation.
\item \textbf{An explanatory link from mechanism and protocol to predictable damage.} Compression
damage is second-order in logit displacement inside an empirical range, and distillation damage is
set jointly by training exposure, student and evaluation distribution, with a reuse cost that
transfers across distributions while the gain does not; these conditions bound where predictive relations hold and turn compression choice into a
validated selection problem for each capability and storage budget, one that pays where a
capability responds to added data and is neutral elsewhere.
\end{itemize}
