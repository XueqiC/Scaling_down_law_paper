\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit, at densities inside the fitted range, the frozen
pruning power form reaches 0.24, 0.24 and 0.68 nats for math, code and QA against 0.23, 0.22 and 0.68
for the per-density regression and 0.28, 0.21 and 0.22 for the median curve: it matches the
regression on math with a quarter of the parameters, trails the median on code, and loses QA to
every source-free curve; at $d=0.55$, outside the range, every source-conditioned form fails by 0.6
to 4.5 nats. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen after development on all 54 cells,
unseen group sizes 32 and 512 on the development states are predicted best by the piecewise
interpolation for math and code (0.07 and 0.12 nats), while on a state absent from the fit the
median is best for math and QA (0.13) and the interpolation for code (0.17 against 0.22); the
per-channel predictor's one unseen setting is worse than a per-bit constant. For distillation, at six
new pools of $U{=}200$ and three new budgets every selected form beats zero change, the gain over the
development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students (0.004 and 0.005 nats) and for
QA on the 270M student only, and the four corner budgets of the rectangle test landed on their
predicted supervised totals to the token (\S\ref{sec:why_shape}).

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

Inside the range, the frozen per-density pruning regression beats its no-$D_0$ variant and the
per-setting median at an unseen stage, and for a 1B source at two new steps the power form, the
regression and the median curve lie within 0.05 nats of one another. Outside it, at a 6.9B source
five times the development range, the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on
math (0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code, the per-bit quantization
regression is worse than the per-bit median at every bit-width, and on a 2.8B source inside the size
range the frozen median density curve predicts within 0.08, 0.05 and 0.07 nats while both
source-conditioned forms are three to seven times worse (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}). For
distillation the 4B student, 6.6 times the reference size and a development student in the final
round, keeps only its QA gain at the development pools (0.83, $[0.12,1.54]$) and is beaten by the
frozen constant at the unseen pool on every capability (Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}).

\paragraph{The range each relation has earned.} Figure~\ref{fig:generalization} separates the three
kinds of novelty. New configurations of seen states are where the initial-state inputs pay: unseen
pruning densities inside the fitted range and unseen quantization group sizes on development states
are predicted within their stated error, and the four distillation corner budgets landed on their
predicted supervised totals to the token. New sources and students are where they do not: at five
times the development size the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to a median curve, a 2.8B source
inside the size range is predicted best by the frozen median density curve, and the 4B distillation
student, drawn as a development student since it entered this round's fits, keeps only its QA gain.
New evaluation distributions split by sign: the reuse cost reproduces on all three, the gain on one.
A relation from this paper should be used inside the panel it was tested on and with a one-point
calibration when the state is new; beyond that its status is a recommendation, not a prediction.
