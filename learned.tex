\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

This study developed predictive scaling-down laws for capability-conditioned loss under pruning,
quantization and distillation, and held each candidate to one validation standard. Under that
standard the survivors are method-specific and bounded: a five-parameter pruning form for math and
code inside its density range, a same-input interpolation over the measured quantization grid, and
one-coefficient reuse forms for distillation on the development students, each carrying the range
on which it was tested and a calibration cost stated in advance (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}).
The structures that failed are reported with the evidence that excluded them: a logarithmic reuse
term contradicted its own flatness implication, dense source statistics fitted without
transferring, and a fit-free rectangle test on two students neither rejected nor established
additivity of budget and reuse. A second-order displacement account explains the measured damage
across three families inside a magnitude boundary without predicting it. The relations feed a
capability- and budget-specific selection rule validated prospectively on four fresh states, and
each relation, verdict and recommendation keeps the status it had before its outcome was measured.
The lessons for building such laws, the guidance it supports for experiment design and
configuration choice, and the limits of the present relations are collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_learned}.
