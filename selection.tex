\section{From Laws to Capability-Specific Selection}
\label{sec:selection}


The relations turn configuration choice into a computation: given a reference model, a capability
and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ within budget $B$. On the controlled panel the law-based choice selects the method of the measured best candidate in
96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells for math, code and QA. A rule was then locked and its maps frozen
for four fresh Pythia states, where they come within 0.14 nats of the best candidate and below
quantization-only under the same candidates (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main}). Those distillation
candidates were historical, so a second confirmation used candidates trained in the same round.

Four references, none of which supplied a measurement to the rule, each received twelve candidates
at seventeen storage budgets: four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the dense model,
and one new student distilled under the recipe on which the delivered relation was fitted. Two are Pythia checkpoints at a training stage with no earlier recorded use; the two Gemma-3 models
carry weights that earlier development measured under other interventions, so there the round tests
new candidates on familiar weights (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). The learned weights of every reference and student base were checked against all thirty registered
earlier revisions, and the predicted loss of every candidate and both maps were sealed before any
student was trained or any candidate compressed. Define the opportunity at a budget as the loss of the best feasible quantization candidate minus
that of the best feasible candidate of any method, and the regret of a policy as the loss of its
own choice minus that same candidate. Question answering carries an opportunity of 0.12 to 0.89 nats, and the best candidate there is the
new student in 59 of the 68 cells that four references and seventeen budgets make; the rule selects
it in 63 and gives up 0.000 nats on three references and 0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to 1.84
nats for quantization-only, whose selection error inside the quantization family adds to the
opportunity it forgoes. Comparisons use the 16 budgets per reference at which both policies can select. For mathematics, code and the largest capability increase the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats, so
quantization already supplies a near-best candidate, the rule matches it, and in three of the 256 paired cells, all at the budget that admits the dense
model, exceeds its regret by at most 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the registered criterion.

A distilled student is also a smaller model, so a retrospective analysis adds the student before
distillation to the same candidate set. It leaves 0.084 to 0.650 nats of the question-answering opportunity to
the training itself and 0.000 to 0.235 to the smaller model alone, while on mathematics and code
the distilled student is worse than the one it started from and none of the small opportunity there
is owed to the distillation data (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-retrospective-control}). Cross-method selection therefore repays its measurements for a capability that responds to added
data, and is neutral for capabilities that do not.
