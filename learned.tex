\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

In this paper, we present a systematic investigation of capability scaling-down laws for LLM compression across varioussettings. Through a common framework and controlled experiments, we characterize how capability losses vary with model state, compression configuration, and training data, and identify shared response patterns and capability differences. Building on these findings, we develop predictive relations and demonstrate that compact forms can reduce measurement requirements while maintaining useful predictive accuracy. We further introduce and independently evaluate a compression selection method that compares predicted capability losses under nominal storage constraints. Together, these contributions clarify the information needed to predict compression outcomes and provide an empirical foundation for developing capability scaling-down laws, reducing repeated evaluation, and aligning compression choices with application-specific capability requirements.
