\section{From Laws to Capability-Specific Selection}
\label{sec:selection}


Given a reference model, a capability and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ within
budget $B$. 





\paragraph{Prospective confirmation of a frozen rule.} Retrospectively, on the controlled panel the
law-based choice selects the method of the oracle in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells for math, code and
QA. A complete selection rule was then locked (source-free predictors on new states), the predicted loss of each
candidate and the four maps were frozen for four fresh Pythia states (160M, 410M and 1.4B at
step 32k, 1B at 64k; 18 configurations each, weights hashed against each earlier revision), and
only then measured (Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main_v2}). Over 68 cells per objective the frozen-rule maps are
within 0.00001, 0.004 and 0.14 nats of the oracle for math, code and QA, below the source-conditioned
maps (0.015, 0.11, 0.21) and quantization-only under the same candidates (0.007, 0.010, 0.25), meeting
the registered criterion; the rule beats quantization-only on three states and trails it on 160M@32k
(0.22 against 0.13). The math and code margins come largely from the one state with
historical distillation candidates; on the three newly measured states the mean regrets differ by
less than $10^{-5}$ nats.


The prospective result establishes the rule on four Pythia states under nominal storage budgets;
validation across model families with fresh distillation candidates is the natural next step, and
no historical distillation candidate carries a claim beyond that panel of four Pythia states measured prospectively.
