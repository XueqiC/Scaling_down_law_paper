\section{Compression Method Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

This section applies the relations of Section~\ref{sec:laws} to the choice of compression
configuration under a storage budget and tests that choice on references that supplied it no outcome.

\subsection{Selection under Storage Constraints}
\label{sec:selection_rule}

Given a reference model, a capability and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ whose nominal storage, stored matrix
parameters rather than runtime memory or sparse indexing, falls within $B$. The final rule calls
the pruning and quantization relations of Section~\ref{sec:laws} on a seen state and their
source-free medians on a new one; for distillation, whose candidates are students of one fixed
recipe, it calls a regression on the student state for mathematics and pre-specified constants for code
and question answering (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:locked_rule}). The budget and pool forms of Eq.~\ref{eq:kdforms} enter no selection. A workload needing several capabilities at once replaces $\widehat L_{m,c}$ by the largest loss increase
$\max_c[\widehat L_c(M)-L_c(M_0)]$. On the controlled panel the law-based choice selects the measured best method in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells for mathematics, code and question answering, and a rule fixed in advance for four fresh Pythia states comes within 0.14 nats of the best candidate (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_regret}).

Each of the four references received twelve candidates at seventeen storage budgets: four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the
dense model and one student distilled in this round. Two are Pythia checkpoints with no earlier recorded use and two are Gemma-3 models with new candidates (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). The opportunity at a budget is the loss of the best feasible quantization
candidate minus that of the best feasible candidate of any method; a policy's regret is the loss of
its choice minus that same candidate. Question answering carries an opportunity of 0.12 to 0.89 nats (Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main}a--d). On the 64 cells at which both policies find a feasible candidate, the rule selects the new student in 63. Its regret there is 0.000 nats on three references and 0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to
1.84 nats for a policy restricted to quantization. For mathematics, code and the largest loss increase the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats and the rule matches quantization to within 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the criterion pre-specified for this round. On 384 fresh question-answering items scored after the round, the rule's choice is ahead of the quantization-only choice by 0.47 to 2.43 nats on three references and level on the fourth, and a generation readout with room to answer puts the same students ahead of that choice on exact match on three references, behind on the fourth and level with their initial students, so the gain is in reference-answer likelihood and generation accuracy remains a separate endpoint (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:p1-behaviour}). A retrospective ablation (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-ablation}) shows that the rule's choices
coincide with those of a development-fixed heuristic, the student first for question answering and
the largest feasible quantization otherwise, so they rest on a stable difference between methods
rather than on fine margins; the medians the rule uses add 0.012 nats on mathematics, and even a
perfect quantization selection trails the rule's choice by 0.09 to 0.89 nats on question answering.
