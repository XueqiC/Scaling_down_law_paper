\section{Empirical Analysis of Capability Loss}
\label{sec:observed}

This section reports what the controlled measurements show: which response shapes capabilities share, how the initial model state and the compression configuration move those
responses, and how training data and the evaluation distribution move them under distillation.

\subsection{Compression Responses across Capabilities}
\label{sec:resp_capabilities}

Compression changes capabilities unevenly, and the order in which it changes them is not fixed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On the controlled Pythia panel, mathematics and code follow one
shared shape in density with a capability-specific scale, whereas the question-answering response
changes sign across density, as Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses} shows at every state.

The two compression axes carry different shapes. Under pruning, once the measured first-order
alignment term is subtracted, the residual before the cliff is a power law in the deleted capability
mass, with an exponent that transfers across families. Under quantization the response is instead a
threshold in bit width whose position depends on the group size, so an interpolation over the
measured grid predicts it where a smooth surface does not. Both descriptions read measurements of
the compressed model, so they explain a response rather than predict one; Appendix~\ref{sec:app_mech}
develops them, including the logit-displacement account of the loss change. A predictive relation may therefore share a shape
between mathematics and code with a capability-specific scale, and needs a separate shape for
question answering.

\subsection{Effects of Model State and Compression Configuration}
\label{sec:state_config}

Using Pythia checkpoints whose size and training stage vary independently, we examine how the
initial model state moves the response. Parameter count and pretraining tokens enter the pruning
amplitude for mathematics and code and carry those capabilities to unseen densities of states that
entered the fit; the same inputs over-predict question answering, whose density response is not
proportional to any shared curve. The state inputs earn their place inside the development range,
where they and the median density curve agree to within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B model,
and lose it outside it (Section~\ref{sec:general}). Under quantization the configuration acts through two
axes at once: the threshold in bit width moves with the group size. A relation therefore needs the configuration in full, and the
initial state only where it helps.

\subsection{Effects of Training Data and Evaluation Distribution}
\label{sec:why}

Distillation depends on the training protocol as strongly as on the method itself. With every other
setting held fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss increase on mathematics and code
grows by an order of magnitude between learning rates of $5\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$; at
the lower rate the 1B student is almost unharmed on those two capabilities and question answering
improves by $0.79$ nats.

Once the learning rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates and question answering bears
most of the cost. At a matched budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens
(Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$ near twelve costs question answering $+0.29$,
$+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats on students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters, and nine times
more independent data converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each.

The reuse cost transfers across question-answering distributions; the gain does not. Rescored on a
fresh 2Wiki sample disjoint from the probe, on MuSiQue and on TriviaQA, all three distributions
worsen at high reuse and improve as reuse falls, on each of the three students at the matched
budget of Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}. Only the fresh 2Wiki sample crosses into a gain there:
MuSiQue flattens near zero and TriviaQA remains positive at every reuse level we scored.

Two pre-specified checks bound how this response may be written. A joint form with separate
logarithmic pool and budget terms implies a pool-change effect that is flat in budget; for code on
the 1B student it drifts by a factor of twenty (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:drift}). A four-trajectory rectangle replaying the trainer's update boundaries then tested additivity
directly: its second difference, zero under any additive structure, lands inside the pre-specified
band on question answering for both students, neither rejecting nor establishing additivity
(Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:corner_test}). Neither check licenses the joint structure, and neither is
the basis for the delivered form. That basis is the development comparison: under
leave-one-run-out, the one-parameter logarithm of the reuse ratio was selected for mathematics and
code and the joint budget and pool form only for question answering (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:distill_confirm}); the logarithmic reuse term is therefore a local approximation at
the tested budgets and pools, with no claim of additive joint structure.

These responses can be turned into an empirical data requirement. A pre-specified experiment of
eighteen trajectories on the 1B and 4B students, with pools from nine thousand to 158 thousand
supervised tokens and three evaluation budgets, measured the range of independent data at which a
loss constraint is met (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:dreq}). The constraint is crossed inside the tested
pools on the fresh 2Wiki sample for both students at the two larger budgets; on MuSiQue a crossing
appears only at looser tolerances, and on TriviaQA the largest pool fails for the 4B student at
every tolerance, so there the requirement is a lower bound. A low-degree boundary relation did not
predict these ranges better than a per-student monotone interpolation, so we report the requirement
as measured intervals, which hold for the data source, the maximum pool size and the protocol
used here.

These observations motivate predictive relations that account for the compression configuration
while allowing responses to differ across capabilities.
