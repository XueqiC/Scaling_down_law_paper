\section{What Compression Does to Capabilities}
\label{sec:observed}

Compression damages capabilities unevenly, and the order in which it damages them is not fixed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On the controlled Pythia panel, mathematics and code follow one
shared shape in density with a capability-specific scale, whereas the question-answering response
changes sign across density, as Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses} shows at every state.

The state of the source model shapes this response, but only within a range. Parameter count and
pretraining tokens enter the pruning amplitude for mathematics and code, and neither improves the
question-answering prediction. Inside the development range the source-conditioned forms and the median density curve agree to
within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B source, while far outside it the source-conditioned forms
are the less accurate of the two (\S\ref{sec:general}).

Distillation adds a dependence on the training protocol that is as strong as the dependence on the
method itself. With every other setting held fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss
increase on mathematics and code grows by an order of magnitude between learning rates of
$5\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$. At the lowest of these rates the 1B student is almost unharmed on those two capabilities, and it still gains $0.79$ nats on question answering, the largest gain in the pilot.

Once the learning rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates, and question answering bears
most of the cost. At a
matched budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a pool reused about
twelve times, a reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$ near twelve, costs question answering $+0.29$, $+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats on
students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters, and nine times more independent data
converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each student. Across the same students and budget, two pool seeds per cell place the noise from the pool draw an
order of magnitude below this reuse cost on every capability.

The reuse cost transfers across question-answering distributions; the gain does not. Rescored on a
fresh 2Wiki sample disjoint from the probe, on MuSiQue and on TriviaQA, all three distributions
worsen at high reuse and improve as reuse falls, for each student. Only the fresh 2Wiki sample crosses into a gain, because MuSiQue flattens near zero and TriviaQA
remains positive at every reuse level, on both students and at every budget we scored.

These responses can be turned into an empirical data requirement. A registered experiment of
eighteen trajectories on the 1B and 4B students, with pools from nine thousand supervised tokens to
the full pool of 158 thousand and three evaluation budgets, measured the range of independent data
at which a loss constraint is met (Fig.~\ref{fig:dreq}). On 2Wiki the constraint is crossed inside the tested pools for both students at the two larger
budgets. On MuSiQue the largest pool still fails at tight tolerances, and a crossing appears only
at looser ones. On TriviaQA the largest pool fails for the 4B student at every tolerance, so the
requirement is a lower bound. A low-degree boundary relation did not predict these ranges more accurately than a per-student
monotone interpolation. We therefore report the requirement as measured intervals, which hold for the data source, the
maximum pool size and the protocol used in these experiments.

Together these observations fix what a law must carry: a capability-specific shape under pruning, a
configuration-dependent threshold under quantization, and a distillation reuse cost that grows with
reuse and with student size and separates by evaluation distribution in sign.
Section~\ref{sec:why} asks what accounts for these features, and \S\ref{sec:laws} builds the
relations on them.
