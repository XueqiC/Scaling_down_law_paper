\section{Empirical Analysis of Capability Loss}
\label{sec:observed}

This section reports what the controlled measurements show: which response shapes capabilities share, how the initial model state and the compression configuration move those
responses, and how training data and the evaluation distribution move them under distillation.

\subsection{Compression Responses across Capabilities}
\label{sec:resp_capabilities}

Compression changes capabilities unevenly and in no fixed order (Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On
the controlled Pythia panel, mathematics and code share one shape in density with a
capability-specific scale, whereas the question-answering response changes sign across density at
every state (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses}).

The two compression axes carry different shapes. Under pruning, once the measured first-order
alignment term is subtracted, the residual before the cliff is a power law in the deleted capability
mass, with an exponent that transfers across families. Under quantization the response is instead a
threshold in bit width whose position depends on the group size, so an interpolation over the
measured grid predicts it where a smooth surface does not. Both read measurements of the compressed model, so they explain rather than predict
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_mech}).

\subsection{Effects of Model State and Compression Configuration}
\label{sec:state_config}

On Pythia checkpoints whose size and training stage vary independently, parameter count and
pretraining tokens enter the pruning amplitude for mathematics and code and carry those capabilities to unseen densities of states that
entered the fit; the same inputs over-predict question answering, whose density response is not
proportional to any shared curve. The state inputs earn their place inside the development range and lose it outside it
(Section~\ref{sec:general}). Under quantization the configuration acts through two axes at once, the threshold in bit width
moving with the group size, so a relation needs the configuration in full and the initial state only
where it helps.

\subsection{Effects of Training Data and Evaluation Distribution}
\label{sec:why}

Distillation depends on the training protocol as strongly as on the method itself. With every other
setting held fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss increase on mathematics and code
grows by an order of magnitude between learning rates of $5\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$; at
the lower rate the 1B student is almost unharmed on those two capabilities and question answering
improves by $0.79$ nats.

Once the learning rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates and question answering bears
most of the cost. At a matched budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens
(Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$ near twelve costs question answering $+0.29$,
$+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats on students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters under the fixed training protocol, and nine times
more independent data converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each.

The reuse cost transfers across question-answering distributions; the gain does not. Rescored on a fresh 2Wiki sample, on MuSiQue and on TriviaQA at the matched budget of
Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}, all three worsen at high reuse and improve as reuse falls, but only the
fresh 2Wiki sample crosses into a gain.

Two pre-specified checks bound how this response may be written: a joint form with separate
logarithmic pool and budget terms implies a pool-change effect that is flat in budget, and for code on
the 1B student it drifts by a factor of twenty (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:drift}); a four-trajectory
rectangle replaying the trainer's update boundaries tested additivity directly, and its second difference, zero under additivity, lands inside the pre-specified band on question
answering for both students, neither rejecting nor establishing additivity (Appendix
Fig.~\ref{fig:corner_test}). The delivered form therefore rests on the development comparison: leave-one-run-out selected the
one-parameter logarithm of the reuse ratio for mathematics and code and the joint budget and pool form only for question answering (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:distill_confirm}), a local approximation at the tested budgets and pools that claims no additive joint structure.

These responses can be turned into an empirical data requirement. A pre-specified experiment of
eighteen trajectories on the 1B and 4B students, with pools from nine thousand to 158 thousand
supervised tokens and three evaluation budgets, measured the range of independent data at which a
loss constraint is met (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:dreq}): it is crossed inside the tested pools on the
fresh 2Wiki sample for both students at the two larger budgets, on MuSiQue only at looser tolerances,
and on TriviaQA not at all for the 4B student, where the requirement is a lower bound. A low-degree
boundary relation did not predict these ranges better than a per-student monotone interpolation, so
we report the requirement as measured intervals.
