\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed language-model agents are specialized systems
\citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,jimenez2024swebench,liu2024agentbench} whose cost scales
with each token. The model that serves such a workload is usually a reference model reduced by
pruning, quantization, or distillation, and choosing the method and its strength currently requires
measuring each candidate. A single compression ratio does not replace that search, because
compression does not reduce capabilities uniformly: across twelve public models the same pruning
density leaves QA the least damaged capability in some series and the most damaged in others
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}), and the ordering changes with size and family.

Existing compression laws predict an aggregate loss from sparsity, precision, training format, the
pre-pruning loss or the teacher loss
\citep{frantar2023scaling,kumar2024scaling,panferov2025unified,zhang2024scaling,busbridge2025distillation},
and studies of uneven damage document it for single tasks or single quantizers
\citep{jaiswal2023compressing,liu2025quantization,sengupta2025compression}. Three limitations
remain: the endpoint is aggregate, so a capability-specific response shape stays invisible; the
fits mix model states that differ in size and pretraining stage, so it is unclear which inputs a
relation may use when the state is new; and coefficients are rarely frozen before the test
measurements. A predictive law for capability-conditioned loss therefore needs its own endpoint, a
panel that separates size from stage, and a validation standard fixed in advance.

We ask when the loss of a capability after compression can be predicted from the initial model
state and the configuration alone. The endpoint is the conditional loss on a fixed probe set
relative to a reference measured beforehand (Eq.~\ref{eq:interface}); the admitted inputs are the
initial state (parameter count, pretraining tokens, reference capability losses) and the
intervention setting, with no measurement of the compressed target. The study then proceeds in one
sequence: controlled measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and stage vary independently,
and on Gemma students under one distillation protocol, establish what a law must reproduce
(\S\ref{sec:observed}); an analysis of shape, conditions and mechanism explains why
(\S\ref{sec:why}); method-specific relations are built on the verified findings
(\S\ref{sec:laws}), tested with coefficients and predictions frozen in advance
(\S\ref{sec:general}), and carried into a selection rule confirmed prospectively
(\S\ref{sec:selection}). Each relation keeps
the status assigned before its outcome was measured, and the findings are stated below as rules
with the measured cases as their instances.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A law may share a response shape only across capabilities whose shapes agree;
capability conditioning is a structural choice and not a scale factor.} Under pruning, math and
code share one curve with capability-specific scales while question answering changes sign across
density, so no rescaled shared curve fits it; a coefficient-free second-order account of logit
displacement explains the measured damage inside an empirical range.
\item \textbf{Interpolating within a measured model and transferring to a new model state are
different prediction problems that admit different inputs.} Initial-state inputs pay for unseen
configurations of measured states; for new states a source-free configuration curve is as good or
better, and the transferable information must be validated on its own, as the frozen tests and a
fit-free rectangle design show.
\item \textbf{The data requirement of distillation is a joint property of training exposure,
student and evaluation distribution, and the cost of reuse transfers where the gain does not.}
Under one protocol the learning rate sets the size of the damage, the reuse cost grows with reuse
and student size and reproduces across three question-answering distributions, and the net gain
appears on one of them.
\item \textbf{What these rules deliver.} Method-specific relations with their tested ranges
(Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}), a selection rule whose frozen maps on four fresh Pythia
states match the measured oracle within 0.00001, 0.004 and 0.14 nats for math, code and QA, and a
released suite of coefficients, registered predictions, measurements and code.
\end{itemize}
