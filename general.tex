\subsection{Measurement Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

Sharing response structure across densities reduces the measurements needed to fit the pruning predictor while retaining coverage of model states. This finding is reproduced in three evaluations (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency-body}, Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}): magnitude pruning on Pythia, magnitude pruning on OLMo-2, and Wanda pruning on Pythia. With coefficients refitted on each development panel, the compact form uses half the configuration measurements and yields math and code errors within 0.020 nats per token of the full-grid regression\rev{, inside the pre-specified tolerances of 0.05 nats on Pythia and 0.020 on OLMo-2 and Wanda}. The reduced grids require 59\% of the full-grid GPU time on OLMo-2 and 70\% under Wanda, including reference measurements, loading, and calibration.

\rev{Three features of the design make this saving informative.} The reduction is in density measurements per state: every
reduced grid keeps all development states, so the saving comes from sharing the density response
rather than from dropping states; at 9 measurements the per-density regression cannot be fitted at all
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). \rev{In a retrospective subsampling, the compact form's median error barely moves between 18 and 36 measurements, so half the grid already sits where further measurements stop paying (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:a9}).} \rev{The comparison carries information where pruning produces a response: under Wanda the 1B and 1.4B test states move by at most 0.06 nats, so no predictor can improve on zero change there, and the saving is established on the states that respond} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_a11}). The errors
are compared on independent panels and are not tests of statistical equivalence. Among forms of
similar complexity fitted on the same half grid, the compact form has the lowest mathematics and code
error in all three evaluations. \rev{Three kinds of transfer are thus separated: the research method applies to all three compression methods, the predictive form and fitting strategy reproduce across two families and two pruning criteria, and the coefficients are estimated for each setting, as scaling-law coefficients generally are \citep{besiroglu2024chinchilla}.}

read the initial state help at unseen configurations of seen states, while on new model states the

development and 21 pre-specified test cells, the piecewise interpolation is the most accurate
predictor of unseen group sizes at the development states for mathematics and code, cutting the error
of the strongest development baseline from 0.333 to 0.065 nats on mathematics and from 0.678 to 0.116
on code. For distillation,
at six new pools and three new budgets, every selected form improves on zero change, with a detected
gain over the development baseline for code on both students and for question answering on the
smaller one. On three Pythia checkpoints in no fit, the pre-specified pruning power form matches the per-density regression on mathematics with a quarter of the parameters \rev{(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results})}. At 2.8B and 6.9B, beyond the development range, the
pre-specified median curve predicts pruning and per-bit quantization more accurately (at 6.9B and step 112k,
0.05, 0.07 and 0.15 nats against 0.23, 0.19 and 0.96 for the source regression)\rev{, which is why the median curve is the rule for new states}.
