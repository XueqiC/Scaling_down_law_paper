\subsection{Measurement Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

Sharing response structure across densities reduces the measurements needed to fit the pruning predictor while retaining coverage of model states. This finding is reproduced in three evaluations (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency-body}, Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}): magnitude pruning on Pythia, magnitude pruning on OLMo-2, and Wanda pruning on Pythia. With coefficients refitted on each development panel, the compact form uses half the configuration measurements and yields math and code errors within 0.020 nats per token of the full-grid regression. The reduced grids require 59\% of the full-grid GPU time on OLMo-2 and 70\% under Wanda, including reference measurements, loading, and calibration. These results support reuse of the predictive form and fitting strategy, with coefficients estimated for each setting.

Three conditions qualify this finding. The reduction is in density measurements per state: every
reduced grid keeps all development states, so the saving comes from sharing the density response
rather than from dropping states; at 9 measurements the per-density regression cannot be fitted at all
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). Where the response is near zero, complex prediction does not beat zero
change: under Wanda the 1B and 1.4B test states move by at
most 0.06 nats and no predictor improves on zero change there (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_a11}). The errors
are compared on independent panels and are not tests of statistical equivalence. Among forms of
similar complexity fitted on the same half grid, the compact form has the lowest mathematics and code
error in all three evaluations. Three kinds of transfer are thus separated: the research
method, with its inputs, measurement budget and independent validation, applies to all three
compression methods; the predictive form and fitting strategy reproduce across two families and two
pruning criteria; and the coefficients are estimated for each setting, since without refitting the
Pythia coefficients err by 0.4 to 1.9 nats on OLMo-2 and the magnitude-pruning coefficients by 0.19 to
0.27 nats under Wanda.

\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Configurations}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

In grouped quantization, with 54 development and 21 pre-specified test cells, the piecewise
interpolation is the most accurate predictor of unseen group sizes at the development states for
mathematics and code. For distillation, at six new pools and three new budgets, every selected form
improves on zero change, with a detected gain over the development baseline for code on both
students and for question answering on the smaller one.

\subsection{Transfer across Model States}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

On three Pythia checkpoints in no fit,
the pre-specified pruning power form matches the per-density regression on mathematics with a quarter of
the parameters, and trails the median on code and every source-free curve on question answering; outside the tested density range every source-conditioned form errs by 0.6 to
4.5 nats. At 2.8B and 6.9B, beyond the development range, the pre-specified median curve predicts pruning and per-bit quantization more accurately, and on a new grouped quantization state it wins for mathematics and question answering and the interpolation for code.
