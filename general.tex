\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Configurations}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

In grouped quantization, with 54 development cells and 21 pre-specified test cells at unseen group
sizes and a new state, the piecewise interpolation is the
most accurate predictor of unseen group sizes at the development states for mathematics and code.
For distillation, at six new pools and three new budgets, every selected form improves on zero
change, and the gain over the development baseline is detected for code on both students and for
question answering on the smaller student alone.

\subsection{Transfer across Model States}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit,
the pre-specified pruning power form matches the per-density regression on mathematics with a quarter of
the parameters, and is less accurate than the median on code and than every source-free curve on
question answering; outside the tested density range every source-conditioned form errs by 0.6 to
4.5 nats. At a 6.9B model, five times the development range, and at a 2.8B one, the pre-specified median
curve is the more accurate predictor for pruning and per-bit quantization alike, by a factor of three
to seven on the 2.8B state. On a new grouped
quantization state the median wins for mathematics and question answering and the interpolation for
code, and the 4B student keeps only its question-answering gain at the development pools.

\subsection{Measurement Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

A subsampling study, with the sampling plan fixed in advance, shows what halving the development
measurements costs: at 18 density measurements per capability the five-parameter form predicts new
states to 0.20 nats on mathematics and 0.17 on code, against the 0.18 and 0.13 the per-density
regression reaches with all 36, and at 9 the regression cannot be fitted at all. The saving comes
from measuring fewer densities per source, not fewer sources (Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). A pre-specified confirmation then fixed that form, the full-budget regression and the median curve
before four Pythia states outside every earlier fit were pruned at six densities. The half-budget
form stayed within 0.013 nats of the full-budget regression on mathematics and 0.020 on code,
meeting the pre-specified criterion, and halved the error of the median curve at the same budget
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}b). That criterion is a point estimate on four
states, not a claim of statistical equivalence, and question answering keeps the median curve as its
delivered predictor; same-input forms with a fixed or quadratic strength dependence are less
accurate at both budgets (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:a14-baselines}).
This is the evidence behind the compact form on new states inside the fitted range; beyond it the
median curve remains the default (Section~\ref{sec:unseen_states}).

Prediction at a new configuration and transfer to a new model state therefore behave differently:
source-conditioned forms support compact prediction inside the controlled pruning domain, and across
broader state changes configuration medians remain the stronger baseline. Either way the prediction
costs no measurement of the compressed model, which is what lets
Section~\ref{sec:selection} compare candidates before any of them is built.
