\section{What Compression Does to Capabilities}
\label{sec:observed}

The measurements show first that damage is uneven across capabilities and that the ordering is
not fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On the controlled Pythia panel, math and code respond to density along one shared shape with a
capability-specific scale, while the QA response changes sign across density, as Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses} shows for every state.

The source state shapes this response inside a range: parameter count and pretraining tokens both
enter the pruning amplitude for math and code, and neither improves the QA prediction.
Inside the development range the source-conditioned forms and the median density curve agree
within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B source; far outside it the source-conditioned forms lose to the median curve, as the tests of \S\ref{sec:general} show for every capability.

Distillation adds a dependence on the protocol as strong as the dependence on the method. With
every other setting fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss increase on math and code
grows by an order of magnitude between learning rates of $5\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$,
while at the lowest rate the 1B student is almost unharmed and still gains $0.79$ nats on QA.

Once the rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates, and QA bears most of the cost. At a
matched budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a pool reused about
twelve times, a reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$ near twelve, costs QA $+0.29$, $+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats on
students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters, and nine times more independent data
converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each student. Two pool seeds per cell put the pool-draw noise an order of magnitude below the reuse cost on the
same students.

The reuse cost also transfers across QA distributions, whereas the gain does not. Scored on a fresh
2Wiki sample, MuSiQue and TriviaQA, all three worsen at high reuse and improve as reuse falls, for
each student, but only 2Wiki crosses into a gain: MuSiQue flattens near zero and TriviaQA stays positive at
every reuse level on both students.

These responses can be turned into an empirical data requirement. In a registered experiment, eighteen trajectories on the 1B and 4B students with pools from nine
thousand supervised tokens to the full pool of 158 thousand, evaluated at three budgets, measured
the range of independent data at which a loss constraint is met (Fig.~\ref{fig:dreq}). On 2Wiki the constraint is crossed inside the tested pools for both students at the two larger
budgets; on MuSiQue the largest pool still fails at tight tolerances and a crossing appears only at
looser ones; on TriviaQA the largest pool fails for the 4B student at every tolerance, so the
requirement is a lower bound there. A low-degree boundary relation did not predict these ranges better than a per-student monotone
interpolation, so the requirement is reported as measured intervals under this data source, pool
size and protocol.

Together these observations fix what a law must carry: a capability-specific shape under pruning,
a configuration-dependent threshold under quantization, and a distillation reuse cost that is
monotone in $E=T/D_U$, grows with student size and separates by evaluation distribution in sign.
Section~\ref{sec:why} asks what accounts for these features and \S\ref{sec:laws} builds on them.
