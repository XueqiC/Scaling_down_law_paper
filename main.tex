\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads require specific capabilities, and the models that serve them are usually
reference models reduced by pruning, quantization, or distillation. We study, with one
capability-conditioned endpoint and one validation standard across the three methods, when the loss
of a capability after compression can be predicted from the initial model state and the
configuration alone. Controlled measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and pretraining stage
vary independently, and on Gemma students under a fixed distillation protocol, yield three findings.
Capabilities differ in the shape of their response: under pruning the question-answering response
changes sign across density and cannot be a scaled copy of the shared math and code curve, which
decides what structure a law may share. Predicting new configurations of measured models and
transferring to new model states are different tasks: initial-state inputs carry math and code to
unseen densities and group sizes, while a source-free configuration curve is the safer predictor for
new states. The data requirement of distillation depends jointly on training exposure, student and
evaluation distribution: the cost of reusing a small pool grows with the reuse ratio and the student
size and reproduces across three question-answering distributions, whereas the net gain is confined
to one of them. On these findings we build method-specific relations, each with its tested range, test them with
coefficients and predictions frozen before measurement, and connect them to a capability- and
budget-specific selection rule confirmed prospectively on eight fresh model states of two families,
where it captures an opportunity of up to 0.89 nats that quantization alone cannot reach. A registered
confirmation shows that the compact pruning form fitted on half of the development configuration
measurements stays within 0.02 nats of the full-budget regression on four further model states, and
a registered distillation experiment measures the range of independent data at which a
capability-specific loss constraint is met. We
release coefficients, registered predictions, measurements and code at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/scaling_down_law}.
\end{abstract}
