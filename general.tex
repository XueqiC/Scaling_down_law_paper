The relations of \S\ref{sec:laws} are now tested on new configurations of seen states and on new

\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

We first evaluate prediction at unseen compression configurations and distillation settings.  Section~\ref{sec:efficiency} separately examines whether a compact pruning relation can reduce development measurements while predicting held-out model states. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all
54 cells, the piecewise interpolation is the most accurate predictor of unseen group sizes at the
development states, for mathematics and for code. For distillation, at six new pools and
three new budgets, every selected form improves on zero change, and the gain over the development
baseline is detected for code on both students and for question answering on the smaller student
alone.

\subsection{Prediction with Fewer Configuration Measurements}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The value of the compact pruning form lies in how few development measurements it requires. A
subsampling study, with the sampling plan fixed in advance, shows what halving them costs: at 18
density measurements per capability the five-parameter form predicts new states to 0.20 nats on
mathematics and 0.17 on code, against the 0.18 and 0.13 that the per-density regression reaches
with all 36, and at 9 the regression cannot be fitted at all. The saving comes from measuring fewer
densities per source, not fewer sources (Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). A registered confirmation then froze that form, the full-budget regression and the median curve
before four Pythia states outside every earlier fit were pruned at six densities. The half-budget
form stayed within 0.013 nats of the full-budget regression on mathematics and within 0.020 on
code, meeting the registered criterion, and halved the error of the median curve at the same budget
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}b). The criterion is a point estimate on four states,
and for question answering the median curve remains the delivered predictor.

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

The second kind of novelty is a new model state. On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit,
the frozen pruning power form matches the per-density regression on mathematics with a quarter of
the parameters, but is less accurate than the median on code and than every source-free curve on
question answering; outside the tested density range, every source-conditioned form errs by 0.6 to
4.5 nats. On a new quantization state the median is the more accurate predictor for mathematics and question
answering, and the interpolation for code. At a 6.9B source, five times the development range, the source-conditioned pruning forms are less
accurate than the median curve on mathematics and question answering, and the per-bit quantization
regression is less accurate than the per-bit median at every bit-width; on a 2.8B source the frozen
median curve reduces the error by a factor of three to seven against both. For distillation, the 4B student retains only its question-answering gain at the development pools,
and at the unseen pool the frozen constant predicts more accurately. Together, these tests distinguish configuration prediction from model-state transfer. Within the controlled pruning domain, initial-state inputs support compact prediction on new configurations and the held-out states of Section 6.2. Across broader state changes, their advantage is inconsistent and configuration medians remain strong baselines. Distillation introduces a further dependence on evaluation distribution: net gains on 2Wiki do not consistently extend to the other QA sets.
