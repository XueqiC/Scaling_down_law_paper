\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

This paper set out to measure how the capabilities of a language model change under compression
and to predict that change before it is paid for. The measurements answered the first question:
mathematics and code share one response shape under pruning with a capability-specific scale,
question answering follows its own, quantization responds through a threshold set by the
configuration, and the cost of reusing distillation data depends on the student and on the
evaluation distribution. The relations answered the second: written down before their tests, they
hold at unseen configurations and new checkpoints, and inside the controlled pruning domain a
compact relation matches a regression fitted on twice the development measurements, so half the
measurements buy the same accuracy. The selection
rule turned prediction into a decision, recovering on four references the cross-method benefit
that a storage constraint leaves open for question answering, where quantization alone gives up
0.41 to 1.84 nats, and its choices stay ahead of or level with the quantization-only choice on
fresh items. A single-method law neither compares methods nor states what it costs to fit; the
relations delivered here do both.
