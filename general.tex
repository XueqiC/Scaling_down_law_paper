\section{Independent Tests: Configurations, States, Capabilities}
\label{sec:general}

The relations of \S\ref{sec:laws} are now tested on new configurations of seen states and on new
model states, with predictions frozen before measurement; Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2} gives
the errors and Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} shows the same comparison for each row of the table.



\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

The first kind of novelty is a new configuration of a state that entered the fit; for pruning,
\S\ref{sec:efficiency} tests the power form at this task. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all 54 cells, unseen group sizes on the development states
are predicted best by the piecewise interpolation for math and code (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}
lists candidates and delivered rule on the same cells). For distillation, at six
new pools and three new budgets each selected form beats zero change and the gain over the development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students and for QA on the 270M student only (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}).

\subsection{Prediction with Fewer Configuration Measurements}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The value of the compact pruning form lies in how few development measurements it needs. A subsampling study with the sampling plan fixed in advance found that the five-parameter form
reaches 0.25 nats on new states with 18 density measurements per capability where the per-density
regression needs 36; the gain comes from measuring fewer densities per source, not fewer sources
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). A registered confirmation then froze the form fitted on
half of the development configuration measurements, the full-budget regression and the median curve
before four Pythia states outside every earlier fit were pruned at six densities. The half-budget
form stayed within 0.013 nats of the full-budget regression on math and 0.020 on code, meeting the
registered criterion, and halved the error of the median curve at the same measurement budget
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}b). This is a point-estimate criterion on four states; for QA the median curve remains the predictor.

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

The second kind of novelty is a new model state. On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit,
the frozen pruning power form matches the per-density regression on math with a quarter of the
parameters, trails the median on code, and loses QA to each source-free curve; outside the range
every source-conditioned form fails by 0.6 to 4.5 nats. On a new quantization state the median wins for math and QA and the interpolation for code. At a 6.9B source five times the development range the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the
median curve on math and QA, and the per-bit quantization regression trails the per-bit median at
every bit-width; on a 2.8B source the frozen median curve predicts within 0.08 nats, three to seven
times better than both source-conditioned forms. For distillation the 4B student keeps only its QA gain at the development pools and loses to the
frozen constant at the unseen pool.

Calibration on one target measurement, a K1 budget distinct from the K0 predictions above, helped
in quantization and was unstable for pruning, where the calibration point carried little damage
signal to estimate from.
