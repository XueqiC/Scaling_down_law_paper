\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed language-model agents are specialists
\citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,jimenez2024swebench,liu2024agentbench} that pay for every
token. The model that serves such a workload is usually a reference model scaled down by pruning,
quantization, or distillation, and choosing the route and its strength today means measuring every
candidate. A single compression ratio cannot replace that search, because compression does not
shrink capabilities uniformly: across twelve public models the same pruning density leaves QA the
least-damaged capability in some series and the most-damaged in others
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_hetero}).

We therefore ask how the capability-conditioned loss after compression
can be predicted from the initial model state and the configuration of
the method. The endpoint is the conditional loss of each capability on a fixed probe set relative to a
reference measured beforehand (Eq.~\ref{eq:interface}); the admitted inputs are the initial state
(parameter count, pretraining tokens, reference capability losses) and the intervention setting, and
no measurement of the compressed target enters. Relations are developed on a controlled panel of Pythia checkpoints whose size and pretraining
stage vary independently, and tested with coefficients and predictions frozen before new
configurations and states are measured.

The study is organised as one chain: define the prediction problem and its admitted inputs, measure
the responses under control, ask which conditions and mechanisms explain their variation, build the
relations those measurements permit, test them on new configurations, states and distributions, and
carry the survivors into a selection decision. Three kinds of result come out of it. (1)
\emph{Responses and relations}: capability-conditioned damage is structured, and where its shape is
shared across states a bounded relation predicts it, per method, with the tested range stated in the
same breath. (2) \emph{Understanding gained in building
reliable laws}: why a logarithmic reuse term fails and additivity was still not rejected; why source
descriptors fit without transferring; why question answering and code diverge; and what
identifiability demands of a design, since the data that fitted a curvature exponent contained no
rectangle on which the additive and interacting readings could be told apart. (3) \emph{Guidance for experiment design and configuration choice}: which inputs to admit for
which transfer, when a calibrated configuration median is the better default, why the protocol must
be fixed before damage is measured, how to design data-reuse experiments so that the decisive
statistic is computable, and how far a selection rule has been validated. Every relation, verdict and
recommendation keeps the status it was given before the outcome was measured: development, frozen
prediction, or post-hoc recommendation.
