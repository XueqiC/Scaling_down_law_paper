\section{Why the Responses Vary: Conditions and Mechanism}
\label{sec:why}

Section~\ref{sec:observed} established what a law must reproduce; this section asks why the
responses take those forms, because the answer decides which structures a law may share and which
inputs it may use. Three questions follow in turn: the shape of the response along each
configuration axis, the conditions that change it, and the mechanism behind the damage; \S\ref{sec:laws} builds only on the verified causes.




\subsection{Function shape}
\label{sec:why_shape}

Under pruning the pre-cliff response, described with gradient information of the dense model, a
richer input than the initial-state inputs of \S\ref{sec:laws}, is a power law in the deleted capability
mass with a shared exponent that transfers across families; under quantization it is a
bit-width threshold whose position depends on the group size, so an interpolation over the
measured grid predicts the response where a smooth surface does not reproduce it.

Under distillation, a joint form with a logarithmic reuse term,
$\delta=(a+a'z)\log(1+T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})+(b+b'z)\log(1+E)$ with $z$ the standardized student size
and $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$ a reference budget, implies that the pool-change effect divided by its log
bracket is flat in budget; it drifts by a factor of twenty across checkpoints for code on the 1B
student (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:drift}). A free reuse exponent, profiled inside training folds, lies near or above one for each capability
with wide fold ranges (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}a), and neither the logarithmic form, the curved
form nor a single interaction term beats the development-stage strongest baseline on either pre-declared target, so the reuse term is delivered in its logarithmic form rather than a curved one.

Those data cannot say whether additivity itself failed, because a fixed pool and a doubling budget
grid never place two trajectories on the same budget; four trajectories designed by replaying the
update boundaries of the trainer landed on a rectangle to the token. The second difference across
it, zero for any additive structure regardless of its curvature,
was $-0.24$ nats on the 1B student and $-0.21$ on the 4B for the QA probe, inside the pre-registered
band of $\pm0.32$ and between the additive prediction of zero and the $-0.49$ of the frozen interaction
candidate (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:corner_test}). The pre-fixed rule neither rejects nor establishes additivity; the 4B statistic on math and code
fell outside its band at readouts that were registered as underpowered in advance.



\subsection{Source state, protocol, and evaluation distribution}
\label{sec:why_conditions}

Size and stage carry math and code
to unseen densities of seen states and over-predict QA, whose density response is not proportional
to any shared curve; dense pre-compression statistics improve a parametric family in four of nine
pairs and lose to the source-free median curve in nine of nine. Under distillation
the learning rate sets the damage magnitude (\S\ref{sec:observed}); at a fixed rate the reuse ratio
$E=T/D_U$ is the dominant factor among those varied: the QA cost it sets is monotone, grows with
student size, and transfers across the three QA distributions while the gain is confined to 2Wiki,
and a residual dependence on budget, pool composition and student remains. A data requirement phrased as a bound on the loss increase is therefore supportable across
distributions, whereas a gain claim holds only for 2Wiki among the distributions tested.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:why_mech}

For pruning and quantization the loss change under a logit displacement $r$ obeys the exact identity
$\Delta L=(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r]-r_y)+\tfrac12\mathrm{Var}_{\pi}(r)+O(\lVert r\rVert^3)$, where $\pi$ is
the next-token distribution of the reference model and $r_y$ the displacement at the target token,
and its
coefficient-free second-order truncation reproduces the measured damage under pruning, grouped
round-to-nearest and per-channel round-to-nearest quantization with an accuracy that follows the
damage magnitude on the measured panel: under 5\% median relative error below about 0.1 nats and
past 20\% by 0.25 (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}b). The range is empirical, since the remainder is
controlled by the logit displacement and the first- and second-order terms can cancel. Pure logit shrinkage carries about three percent of the response; the residual variance that
carries the rest needs the compressed model, so the account explains the damage after the fact and
enters no relation as an input.
