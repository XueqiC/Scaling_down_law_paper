\section{Independent Tests: Configurations, States, Capabilities}
\label{sec:general}

The relations of \S\ref{sec:laws} are now tested on new configurations of seen states and on new
model states, with predictions frozen before measurement; Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2} gives
the errors and Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} shows the same comparison for each row of the table.



\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

The first kind of novelty is a new configuration of a state that entered the fit. A registered
follow-up on four further Pythia states outside every fit found the five-parameter pruning form
fitted on half the density measurements within 0.02 nats of the full-budget per-density regression on math and code, and 0.04 to 0.07 nats better than the median curve
(Appendix~\ref{tab:a11}). In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all 54 cells, unseen group sizes on the development states
are predicted best by the piecewise interpolation for math and code (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}
lists candidates and delivered rule on the same cells). For distillation, at six
new pools and three new budgets each selected form beats zero change and the gain over the development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students and for
QA on the 270M only (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}).

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

The second kind of novelty is a new model state, and here the range of each relation shows. On
three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit, the frozen pruning power form matches the per-density
regression on math with a quarter of the parameters, trails the median on code, and loses QA to each source-free curve; at $d=0.55$, outside the range, each
source-conditioned form fails by 0.6 to 4.5 nats. On a new quantization state the median wins for
math and QA and the interpolation for code; inside the range the frozen forms and the median curve
agree within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B source. Outside it, at a 6.9B source
five times the development range, the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on
math and QA and tie on code, the per-bit quantization
regression is worse than the per-bit median at each bit-width, and on a 2.8B source inside the size
range the frozen median density curve predicts within 0.08, 0.05 and 0.07 nats, three to seven
times better than both source-conditioned forms. For
distillation the 4B student, 6.6 times the reference size and a development student in the final
round, keeps only its QA gain at the development pools and is beaten by the
frozen constant at the unseen pool on each of the three capabilities.

Taken together, Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} gives each relation its validated range: the
initial-state inputs pay for new configurations of seen states, the source-free median curve is the
safer predictor for new sources and students, and new evaluation distributions split by sign.
Calibration on one target measurement, a K1 budget distinct from the K0 predictions above, helped in
quantization and was unstable for pruning where the calibration point carried little damage signal.
