\section{From Laws to Capability-Specific Selection}
\label{sec:selection}


The relations turn configuration choice into a computation: given a reference model, a capability
and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ within budget $B$. On the controlled panel,
the law-based choice selects the method of the measured best candidate in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289
cells for mathematics, code and question answering. We then locked a complete rule and froze its
maps for four fresh Pythia states, where they come within 0.14 nats of the best candidate and below
quantization alone under the same candidates (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main}). The distillation
candidates in that round were historical, so a second confirmation used candidates trained in the
same round.

Four references, none of which supplied a measurement to the rule, each received twelve candidates
at seventeen storage budgets: four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the dense model
and one new student, distilled under the recipe on which the delivered relation was fitted. Two are Pythia checkpoints at a training stage with no earlier recorded use; the two Gemma-3 models
carry weights that earlier development measured under other interventions, so for them the round
tests new candidates on familiar weights (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). Before any outcome was observed, we compared the learned weights of every reference and student
base against the thirty registered earlier revisions, then sealed every predicted loss and both
selection maps. Define the opportunity at a budget as the loss of the best feasible quantization candidate minus
that of the best feasible candidate of any method, and the regret of a policy as the loss of its
own choice minus that same candidate. Question answering carries an opportunity of 0.12 to 0.89 nats, and the best candidate there is the
new student in 59 of the 68 cells. The rule selects that candidate in 63 of them, and its regret is 0.000 nats on three references and
0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to 1.84 nats for a policy restricted to quantization, whose
selection error inside that family adds to the opportunity it forgoes. Comparisons use the 16
budgets per reference at which both policies select. For mathematics, code and the largest capability increase, the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats.
Quantization already supplies a near-best candidate there, the rule matches it, and in three of the
256 paired cells, all at the budget that admits the dense model, the rule exceeds its regret by at
most 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the criterion registered for this round.

A distilled student is also a smaller model, so a retrospective analysis adds the pristine student
to the same candidate set. It attributes 0.084 to 0.650 nats of the question-answering opportunity to the training itself and
0.000 to 0.235 to the smaller model alone. On mathematics and code the distilled student is worse
than the one it started from, so none of the small opportunity there is owed to the distillation
data (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-retrospective-control}). Cross-method selection therefore repays its measurements for a capability that responds to added
data, and is neutral for the rest.
