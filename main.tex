\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads require specific capabilities, and the models that serve them are usually
reference models reduced by pruning, quantization, or distillation. We study, with one endpoint
and one validation standard across the three methods, the inputs, configurations and evaluation
scopes under which a parsimonious relation predicts the loss conditioned on a capability and an
evaluation distribution, and which plausible structures fail under independent tests. Controlled
measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and pretraining stage vary independently, and on Gemma
students under a fixed distillation protocol, establish the observations that a law must
reproduce: damage that differs across capabilities with an ordering that changes across models, a
dependence on the source state that holds inside a range, distillation damage that depends on the
learning rate, and a cost of reusing a small data pool that grows with the reuse ratio and the
student size and transfers across question-answering distributions although the gain does not. The relations that pass frozen tests are method-specific and bounded: a five-parameter
pruning form for math and code inside its density range, a same-input interpolation over the
measured quantization grid, and one-coefficient reuse forms on the development students. We also identify which
plausible structures fail and why, which narrows the space of admissible laws: a logarithmic reuse
term contradicts its own flatness implication, and a fit-free rectangle test gives the first direct
measurement of the budget-by-reuse interaction. A second-order account explains compression damage
across three families inside a magnitude boundary but does not predict it. The relations feed a
capability- and budget-specific selection problem validated prospectively on four fresh states. We
state the range and calibration cost of each prediction and release coefficients, registered
predictions, measurements and code. The public code repository is
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/scaling_down_law}.
\end{abstract}
