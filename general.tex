\section{Independent Tests: Configurations, States, Capabilities}
\label{sec:general}

The relations of \S\ref{sec:laws} are now tested on new configurations of seen states and on new
model states, with predictions frozen before measurement; Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2} gives
the errors, and Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} compares each row of the table with its own
development-selected baseline.



\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

The first kind of novelty is a new configuration of a state that entered the fit; for pruning,
\S\ref{sec:efficiency} tests the power form at this task. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all 54 cells, the piecewise interpolation
predicts unseen group sizes of the development states best for math and code. For distillation, at
six new pools and three new budgets, each selected form improves on zero change, and the gain over the development baseline is detected
for code on both students and for question answering on the smaller of the two students alone.

\subsection{Prediction with Fewer Configuration Measurements}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The value of the compact pruning form lies in how few development measurements it needs. A subsampling study with the sampling plan fixed in advance shows what half of the measurements
costs: with 18 density measurements per capability the five-parameter form predicts new states to
0.20 nats on math and 0.17 on code against the 0.18 and 0.13 the per-density regression reaches
with all 36, and at 9 the regression cannot be fitted at all. The saving comes from measuring fewer
densities per source, not fewer sources (Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}a). A registered confirmation then froze that form, the full-budget regression and the median curve
before four Pythia states outside every earlier fit were pruned at six densities. The half-budget
form stayed within 0.013 nats of the full-budget regression on math and 0.020 on code, meeting the
registered criterion, and halved the error of the median curve at the same measurement budget
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}b). This is a point-estimate criterion on four states; for QA the median curve remains the predictor.

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

The second kind of novelty is a new model state. On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit,
the frozen pruning power form matches the per-density regression on math with a quarter of the
parameters, is less accurate than the median on code, and is less accurate than every source-free
curve on question answering; outside the range every source-conditioned form errs by 0.6 to 4.5
nats. On a new quantization state the median is the more accurate predictor for math and question
answering, and the interpolation for code. At a 6.9B source five times the development range the source-conditioned pruning forms are less
accurate than the median curve on math and question answering, the per-bit quantization regression
is less accurate than the per-bit median at every bit-width, and on a 2.8B source the frozen median
curve reduces the error three to seven times against both. For distillation the 4B student retains only its question-answering gain at the development pools,
and at the unseen pool the frozen constant is the more accurate predictor.

Together these tests give each relation its validated range: the
initial-state inputs pay for new configurations of seen states, the source-free median curve is the
safer predictor for new sources and students, and new evaluation distributions split by sign.
Calibration on one target measurement, a K1 budget distinct from the K0 predictions above, helped
in quantization and was unstable for pruning, where the calibration point carried little damage
signal to estimate from.
