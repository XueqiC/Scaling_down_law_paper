\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads need specific capabilities, and the models that serve them are usually
reference models scaled down by pruning, quantization, or distillation. We study, with one endpoint
and one validation standard across the three methods, under which inputs, configurations and
evaluation scopes a parsimonious relation predicts the loss conditioned on a capability and an
evaluation distribution, and which plausible structures fail under independent test. Controlled
measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and pretraining stage vary independently, and on Gemma
students under a fixed distillation protocol, fix what any law must reproduce: capability-uneven
damage whose ordering changes across models, a source-state dependence that holds inside a range,
distillation damage set by the learning rate, and a cost of reusing a small data pool that grows with
reuse and student size and transfers across question-answering distributions while the gain does
not. The relations that survive frozen tests are method-specific and bounded: a five-parameter
pruning form for math and code inside its density range, a same-input interpolation over the
measured quantization grid, and one-coefficient reuse forms on the development students. Structures
that did not survive are reported with what excluded them, including a logarithmic reuse term that
fails its own flatness implication and a fit-free rectangle test that neither rejects nor
establishes additivity of budget and reuse. A second-order account explains compression damage
across three families inside a magnitude boundary without predicting it. We connect the relations
to a capability- and budget-specific selection problem validated prospectively on four fresh
states, state the range and calibration cost of every prediction, and release coefficients,
registered predictions, measurements and code. The public code repository is
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/scaling_down_law}.
\end{abstract}
