\section{Compression Method Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

\subsection{Selection under Storage Constraints}
\label{sec:selection_rule}

The relations turn configuration choice into a computation: given a reference model, a capability
and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ whose nominal storage falls within budget
$B$, nominal in that it counts stored matrix parameters and not runtime memory or sparse indexing.
For a workload that needs several capabilities at once we use the largest loss increase,
$\max_c[\widehat L_c(M)-L_c(M_0)]$, as the objective in place of a single $\widehat L_{m,c}$. On the controlled panel,
the law-based choice selects the method of the measured best candidate in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289
cells for mathematics, code and question answering. We then locked a complete rule and froze its
maps for four fresh Pythia states, where they come within 0.14 nats of the best candidate and below
quantization alone under the same candidates (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main}). The distillation
candidates in that round were historical, so a second confirmation used candidates trained in the
same round.

\subsection{Independent Evaluation}
\label{sec:selection_confirm}

Four references, none of which supplied a measurement to the rule, each received twelve candidates
at seventeen storage budgets: four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the dense model
and one new student, distilled under the recipe on which the delivered relation was fitted. Two are Pythia checkpoints at a training stage with no earlier recorded use; the two Gemma-3 models
carry weights that earlier development measured under other interventions, so for them the round
tests new candidates on familiar weights (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). Before any outcome was observed, we compared the learned weights of every reference and student
base against the thirty Pythia revisions in the registered records, then sealed every predicted loss and both
selection maps. Define the opportunity at a budget as the loss of the best feasible quantization candidate minus
that of the best feasible candidate of any method, and the regret of a policy as the loss of its
own choice minus that same candidate. Question answering carries an opportunity of 0.12 to 0.89 nats. Over all 68 question-answering
cells, four references at seventeen budgets each, the measured best candidate is the new student in
59; of the nine remaining cells, three admit no feasible candidate at any method, five prefer
pruning and one quantization. The two policies are compared on the 64 cells, sixteen budgets per
reference, at which both find a feasible candidate, and the rule selects the new student in 63 of
those. Its regret there is 0.000 nats on three references and 0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to
1.84 nats for a policy restricted to quantization, whose selection error inside that family adds to
the opportunity it forgoes. For mathematics, code and the largest loss increase, the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats.
Quantization already supplies a near-best candidate there, the rule matches it, and in three of the
256 paired cells, those same sixteen budgets across four references and four objectives, all at the
budget that admits the dense model, the rule exceeds its regret by at most 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the criterion registered for this round.

To distinguish the contribution of distillation from the value of using a smaller model, we retrospectively include the pristine student as a control at the same nominal storage cost. Replacing the pristine student with its distilled counterpart, with quantization available in both candidate sets, increases the question-answering opportunity by 0.084 to 0.650 nats across the four references. The corresponding opportunity changes for math and code are non-positive (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-retrospective-control}). The quantities are changes in the opportunity available to the candidate set, not the loss change
of a student against its own starting point. This control identifies where distillation creates
additional selection opportunity and where quantization already supplies a competitive candidate.
