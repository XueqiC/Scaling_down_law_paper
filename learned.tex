\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

This study set out to make the capability cost of compression predictable, and it returns three
things. The systematic investigation shows which response shapes capabilities share and which they
do not: mathematics and code admit one shape under pruning with a capability-specific scale, while
question answering needs its own, and under distillation the cost of reusing data depends on the
student and on the evaluation distribution. The predictive relations turn those observations into
forms that can be fitted and tested, and one of them buys its accuracy back in measurements: inside
the controlled pruning domain a compact relation matches a regression fitted on twice as many
development configuration measurements. The selection method carries the predictions into a
decision and shows, on references that supplied no intervention outcome to the rule, that predicted
capability losses recover most of the available cross-method benefit for question answering under
nominal storage constraints.

Three boundaries fix where these results apply. Measurement efficiency and stable transfer are
different properties: the compact pruning form reaches the full-budget regression from half the
development measurements inside the controlled domain, and does not thereby carry to states far
outside it. The configuration range, the student state and the evaluation distribution bound each
relation, which is why the distillation requirement is reported as measured intervals on the
distributions we scored. The selection evidence holds for the candidate sets and the nominal
storage budgets tested, and what it returns depends on the capability: where quantization already
supplies a near-best candidate the cross-method choice has little to add, which is itself part of
the answer.
