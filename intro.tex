\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed language-model agents are specialized systems
\citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,jimenez2024swebench,liu2024agentbench} whose cost scales
with each token. Pruning, quantization, and distillation offer different ways to reduce the cost of serving these workloads. Choosing among them requires predicting how each method and configuration changes the capabilities the workload needs. Compression does not reduce capabilities uniformly: across twelve public models the same pruning
density leaves question answering the least damaged capability in some series and the most damaged in others
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}), and the ordering changes with model size and model family.

Existing compression laws predict an aggregate loss from sparsity, precision, training format, the
pre-pruning loss or the teacher loss
\citep{frantar2023scaling,kumar2024scaling,panferov2025unified,zhang2024scaling,busbridge2025distillation},
and studies of uneven damage document it for single tasks or single quantizers
\citep{jaiswal2023compressing,liu2025quantization,sengupta2025compression}. Three limitations
remain: the endpoint is aggregate, so a capability-specific response shape stays invisible; the
fits mix model states that differ in size and pretraining stage, so it is unclear which inputs a
relation may use when the state is new; and coefficients are rarely frozen before the test measurements.

We ask when the loss of a capability after compression can be predicted from the initial model
state and the configuration alone. The endpoint is the conditional loss on a fixed probe set
relative to a reference measured beforehand (Eq.~\ref{eq:interface}); the admitted inputs are the
initial state, meaning parameter count, pretraining tokens and reference capability losses, and
the intervention setting; no measurement of the compressed target is used. The study then proceeds in one
sequence: controlled measurements on Pythia checkpoints whose size and stage vary independently,
and on Gemma students under one distillation protocol, establish what a law must reproduce
(\S\ref{sec:observed}); an analysis of shape, conditions and mechanism explains why
(\S\ref{sec:why}); method-specific relations are built on the verified findings
(\S\ref{sec:laws}), tested with coefficients and predictions frozen in advance
(\S\ref{sec:general}), and carried into a selection rule confirmed prospectively
(\S\ref{sec:selection}). Each relation keeps the status assigned before its outcome was measured.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Capability-dependent compression responses.} We systematically investigate pruning, quantization, and distillation through capability-conditioned loss. Controlled measurements show where response shapes can be shared and where capability-specific relations are needed. Under pruning, the QA response departs from the proportional shapes that describe math and code. Separate tests distinguish prediction at new configurations from transfer across model states.
\item \textbf{Measurement-efficient predictive relations.} On four independently evaluated model states, a compact pruning predictor fitted on 18 development configuration measurements has math and code mean absolute errors within 0.013 and 0.020 nats, respectively, of a regression fitted on 36 measurements. The subsampling study locates this advantage in reducing density measurements per source while retaining source coverage.
\item \textbf{Empirical data requirements for distillation.} Controlled student, data-pool, and training-budget experiments reveal how reuse penalties depend on student size and evaluation distribution. The QA cost of high reuse reproduces across three distributions, while net improvements remain specific to 2Wiki. We translate these responses into empirical data-requirement intervals and bounds under explicit loss constraints.
\item \textbf{Capability-specific compression selection.} Prospective tests in two model families show that a frozen selection rule captures most of the available cross-method opportunity for QA under nominal storage constraints. A retrospective pristine-student control identifies additional opportunity contributed by distillation. Opportunities on math and code are substantially smaller, explaining where cross-method selection adds value.
\end{itemize}
