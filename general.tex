\section{Independent Tests: Configurations, States, Capabilities}
\label{sec:general}

The relations of \S\ref{sec:laws} are now tested on three kinds of novelty: new configurations of
seen states, new model states, and new capabilities or evaluation distributions. Each test scores
predictions frozen before measurement; Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2} gives the error of each
relation against its development-stage baseline, and Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} shows the same
comparison per row, separated by the kind of novelty each row introduces, from new settings to new states and
distributions.



\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

The first kind of novelty is a new configuration of a seen state. On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit, the frozen pruning power form matches the
per-density regression on math with a quarter of the parameters (0.24 against 0.23 nats), trails the
median on code, and loses QA to each source-free curve; at $d=0.55$, outside the range, each
source-conditioned form fails by 0.6 to 4.5 nats. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all 54 cells, unseen group sizes on the
development states are predicted best by the piecewise interpolation for math and code (0.07 and
0.12 nats), and on a state absent from the fit by the median for math and QA and the interpolation
for code. For distillation, at six
new pools and three new budgets each selected form beats zero change and the gain over the
development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students (0.004 and 0.005 nats) and for
QA on the 270M only (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}).

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

The second kind of novelty is a new model state, and here the range of each relation shows. Inside
the range the frozen forms and the median curve lie within 0.05 nats of one another at
unseen stages of a 1B source. Outside it, at a 6.9B source
five times the development range, the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on
math (0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code, the per-bit quantization
regression is worse than the per-bit median at each bit-width, and on a 2.8B source inside the size
range the frozen median density curve predicts within 0.08, 0.05 and 0.07 nats while both
source-conditioned forms are three to seven times worse. For
distillation the 4B student, 6.6 times the reference size and a development student in the final
round, keeps only its QA gain at the development pools (0.83, $[0.12,1.54]$) and is beaten by the
frozen constant at the unseen pool on each of the three capabilities.

Taken together, Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} gives each relation its validated range. For new
configurations of seen states the initial-state inputs are useful: unseen pruning
densities inside the range and unseen group sizes on development states are predicted within their
stated error. For new sources and students the source-free median curve with a one-point
calibration is the reliable default: at five times the development size it beats the
source-conditioned pruning forms, and the 4B distillation student keeps its QA gain. New evaluation
distributions split by sign, which the relations state explicitly. Each relation therefore comes
with the panel on which it was validated, and a one-point calibration carries it to a new state.
