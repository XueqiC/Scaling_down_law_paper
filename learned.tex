\section{What Was Learned, and What It Enables}
\label{sec:learned}

\paragraph{Responses and relations.} Compression damages capabilities unevenly, and the unevenness is
structured: a capability-specific power form under pruning inside $d\in[0.6,0.9]$, a
configuration-dependent threshold under quantization that the measured grid predicts better than any
surface, and under distillation a reuse cost that is monotone in $E=T/D_U$, grows with student size
and reproduces across QA distributions while the gain does not (\S\ref{sec:observed},
\S\ref{sec:laws}, Table~\ref{tab:main_prediction_v2}).

\paragraph{Understanding gained in trying to build reliable laws.} First, a rejected form is not a rejected class: the four-coefficient joint form with a
logarithmic reuse term failed its own flatness implication, yet the fit-free second-difference test on
two students did not reject additivity of budget and reuse on the QA readout, with the measured
statistic between the additive prediction of zero and the frozen interaction candidate's $-0.49$ nats,
about one and a half noise units from each (\S\ref{sec:why}). Second, an explanation is not a
predictor: the second-order displacement account reproduces damage across three families inside a
magnitude boundary and needs the compressed model to compute. Third, source information transfers less than it fits: dense
pre-compression statistics improve a parametric family in four of nine arm-and-capability pairs and
lose to a source-free median configuration curve in nine of nine, and holding that curve fixed while
adding only a shrunk source correction gains nothing on the primary split. Fourth, identifiability is a
property of the design: the data that fitted a curvature exponent near one contained no
budget-by-reuse rectangle, so the one fit-free statistic that separates additive from interacting
structures was uncomputable until four trajectories were designed to land on the corners.

\paragraph{Guidance for design and choice.} Choose inputs by the transfer needed: the initial state carries math and code at unseen densities
and group sizes of seen states and over-predicts QA under pruning on every new checkpoint, so a
configuration median with a one-point calibration is the safer default when the state is new. Fix the training protocol before measuring distillation damage, because the learning rate
changes the damage magnitude by an order of magnitude on the 1B student while leaving the size
ordering intact. Design data-reuse experiments so that at least one budget-by-reuse rectangle exists by
construction, since fixed pools and a doubling budget grid never produce one. Budget calibration
by what it buys: one calibration point on the target distribution closes most of the gap to a
source-specific fit, and the dense statistics that cost more did not earn it. State the range with
the relation, as Table~\ref{tab:main_prediction_v2} does, and as the decision study does by covering
four Pythia states under nominal storage budgets with its cross-family validation not completed.

\paragraph{Limits, and what would reopen the experiments.} The relations are conditional on the evaluated
distributions and the compression implementations. No fitted joint structure for distillation beat
a low-order response surface on either pre-declared target, so the data requirement a practitioner
would most like, how much independent data keeps a conditional loss below a threshold, is supportable
in form and not yet delivered as a validated relation; and the larger student diverged from the
smaller on math and code at readouts the design could not resolve. Reopening measurement needs a new
information source or a mechanism hypothesis, not another candidate reuse function.
