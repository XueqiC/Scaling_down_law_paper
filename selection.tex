The relations turn configuration choice into a computation: given a reference model, a capability
and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ within
budget $B$. Retrospectively, on the controlled panel the law-based choice selects the method of the oracle in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells for math, code and
QA. A complete selection rule was then locked (source-free predictors on new states), the predicted
loss of each candidate and the four maps were frozen for four fresh Pythia states (160M, 410M and
1.4B at step 32k, 1B at 64k; 18 configurations each), and only then measured
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main_v2}). Over 68 cells per objective the frozen-rule maps are within 0.00001, 0.004 and 0.14 nats of the
oracle for math, code and QA, below the source-conditioned maps (0.015, 0.11, 0.21) and
quantization-only under the same candidates (0.007, 0.010, 0.25), meeting the registered criterion;
the rule trails quantization-only on one state (160M at step 32k, 0.22 against 0.13). The math and code margins come largely from the one state with
historical distillation candidates; on the three newly measured states the rule and the
quantization-only baseline coincide to within $10^{-5}$ nats.


The prospective result establishes the rule on four Pythia states under nominal storage budgets;
validation across model families with fresh distillation candidates is the natural next step, and
no historical distillation candidate carries a claim beyond that panel.
