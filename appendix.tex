\section{Experimental Details}
\label{sec:app_details}
\subsection{Probes and loss computation}
\label{sec:app_probes}

The primary probes draw from MATH-500 \citep{hendrycks2021math,lightman2023verify}, MBPP
\citep{austin2021program}, and 2WikiMultihopQA \citep{ho2020constructing}; the secondary benchmarks of the measurement
checks are GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, SVAMP \citep{patel2021svamp}, HumanEval \citep{chen2021codex}, HotpotQA
\citep{yang2018hotpotqa}, TriviaQA \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}, and MuSiQue \citep{trivedi2022musique}. Models: Pythia
\citep{biderman2023pythia}, Gemma 3 \citep{gemma2025gemma3}, Qwen3 \citep{qwen2025qwen3}; LoRA adapters \citep{hu2022lora}.
The size-and-stage panel design follows the observational use of released checkpoints \citep{ruan2024observational,polo2024tinybenchmarks}.
Each capability is measured on a fixed probe set of prompt--reference
pairs drawn once, with a fixed seed, from held-out benchmark splits that
are disjoint from each training and recovery source used in the paper.
The primary measurement benchmarks are MATH-500 for mathematical
reasoning, MBPP for code generation, and 2WikiMultihopQA for multi-hop
question answering. For each capability we draw 128 probes and split
them by index parity into an estimation half, used only for gradient
and Fisher statistics, and a measurement half of 64 probes on which
each reported loss is computed, so that mechanism statistics and
evaluations do not share sampling noise. Secondary benchmarks used for the measurement checks below are
GSM8K, HumanEval, and HotpotQA on the pruning panel (no training
involved) and SVAMP, HumanEval, and TriviaQA where distilled models are
scored, chosen to exclude the benchmarks from which the distillation
traces were derived; a sample-level audit found no overlap between any
measurement probe and any training pool. The measurement half is 64
probes per capability for every result in the paper except where a
512-probe re-measurement is stated.


\subsection{Compression protocols}
\label{sec:app_compress}
\paragraph{Pruning.} Global magnitude pruning removes the smallest
weights across all transformer matrices (attention and MLP projections;
embeddings, output head, biases, and norms are excluded) to a target
retained density $\dens$. The global threshold is estimated from a
seeded sample of absolute weights so that the same procedure scales to
30B-parameter models; each density is applied to the same dense
reference weights, which are held on the CPU for the largest models so
that a single 80~GB device suffices.

\paragraph{Quantization.} Weights are fake-quantized per output channel
with symmetric round-to-nearest at bit-width $\bits$, using the integer
range $[-(2^{\bits-1}-1),\,2^{\bits-1}-1]$ and a per-channel scale set
by the channel's maximum absolute value; activations are left in
bfloat16. The dense weights are restored between bit-widths. The quantizer is
\texttt{fake\_quantize\_per\_output\_channel} in the released code:
$q_{\max}=2^{\bits-1}-1$ is the largest positive integer, integers are
clamped to $[-q_{\max},q_{\max}]$ ($2^{\bits}-1$ values), and the
scale is the channel's maximum absolute weight divided by $q_{\max}$.
With a fixed clipping range the step size from 4 to 5 bits shrinks by
$7/15$, so the squared step ratio is $(7/15)^2\approx0.22$; in general
the squared step ratio between adjacent bit-widths is
$(q_{\max}(\bits)/q_{\max}(\bits+1))^2$, not a constant $1/4$. A
squared step ratio becomes a loss ratio only under assumptions about
the error distribution and the local response that we do not test, so
the fixed $4^{-\bits}$ shape is one candidate among others rather than a
derived reference. Nominal storage ratios are computed from bit-widths;
no memory or latency was measured.

\paragraph{Distillation.} Students are fine-tuned on black-box teacher
traces (a frontier API teacher answering GSM8K, HotpotQA, and CodeAlpaca
prompts; 600 traces per domain) under the full-trace recipe, with
strict LoRA adapters of rank 16 and scaling 32 on the query, key, value,
output, gate, up, and down projections, AdamW at learning rate
$10^{-4}$, an effective batch of 16 sequences, and a cosine schedule
with 3\% warmup. A run aborts if the adapter library is unavailable and does not fall
back to full fine-tuning silently, and the actual
training mode and trainable parameter count are recorded in each
manifest. Token accounting distinguishes processed tokens (all input
tokens seen, including repetitions), unique pool tokens, and supervised
completion tokens. The data pool of $U$ examples per domain is either
the first $U$ rows of the trace file (the historical protocol) or a
random sample drawn with a pool-sampling seed that is independent of the
training seed; pools are identified by the sorted hashes of their
samples and pairwise overlaps are reported. The historical schedule
spanned a fixed number of epochs, so its cosine horizon varied with
pool size; the controlled matrix of \S\ref{sec:law_distill} uses a schedule
defined on the same absolute processed-token budget for each pool, and
the two protocols are kept apart in fitting.



\paragraph{Grouped quantizer.} The grouped variant (\S\ref{sec:law_quant}) splits each row of a weight matrix into
contiguous groups of $g$ input weights, pads the last group with zeros (which do not affect the group's absmax), and
applies the same symmetric round-to-nearest with $q_{\max}=2^{b-1}-1$ per group; $g$ equal to the row width reproduces
the per-channel quantizer bit-exactly (verified by a unit test). Configurations are $b\in\{3,4,5\}$ and
$g\in\{64,128,256\}$; nominal storage is computed from $b$ and $g$, and no memory or latency was measured.

\subsection{The controlled Pythia panel}
\label{sec:app_pythia}
The panel uses the public Pythia checkpoints at sizes 160M, 410M, and
1.4B and pretraining steps 16{,}000, 64{,}000, and 143{,}000, which
correspond to 33.6B, 134.2B, and 299.9B processed tokens at the
published batch of $1024\times2048$ tokens per step. The size
coordinate $N_0$ counts transformer-matrix parameters only (the query,
key, value, output, and MLP matrices of each layer), computed from the
architecture configuration, and the training-tokens coordinate is
$D_0=\text{step}\times 2{,}097{,}152$. Each checkpoint's weight identity
was verified before use: the 2.8B revisions published for different
steps were found to load identical weights and were excluded, and an
integrity guard now rejects any pair of nominally different checkpoints
with identical measured panels. New sources added for the prospective
tests (Pythia-1B at steps 32{,}000, 96{,}000, and 112{,}000;
Pythia-6.9B at 32{,}000 and 112{,}000) were confirmed to have not
entered any fit or model selection before their predictions were
frozen.

\subsection{Grouped quantization: leave-one-state-out on six states}
\input{tables/v55_loso}

The low-order surface tested for grouped quantization is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,b,g)=\phi(\mathbf x)\cdot\big[1,\,u,\,v,\,uv,\,u^2\big],
\qquad u=\log_2 q_{\max}(b)\ \text{(centred)},\ v=\log_2\frac{g}{128},
\label{eq:quant2d}
\end{equation}
with one four-vector of state coefficients per term (twenty coefficients).

\paragraph{Identifiability of the two-dimensional form.} The
development bit-widths are $b\in\{3,5\}$, so the centred $u$ takes the
two values $\pm1.16$ and $u^2$ equals $1.35$ on every development
cell: the quadratic column is a multiple of the intercept column, the
design of Eq.~\ref{eq:quant2d} has rank 16 for its 20 coefficients,
and the ridge penalty resolves the four undetermined directions (the
effective degrees of freedom are 15.9 per capability;
Table~\ref{tab:quant_ident}). At the unseen $b=4$, where $u\approx0$
and $u^2\approx0$, the prediction depends on how the ridge split the
fitted intercept between the two columns. A same-input design without
the quadratic term spans the same development column space and gives
the same development fit, yet predicts the unseen bit-width far worse
(bit-test MAE 1.30, 1.39, 1.94 against 0.19, 0.21, 0.44), and so do
the bit-only and bit-plus-group additive designs; at $g=128$ all three
reduced designs coincide because the group terms vanish. The frozen
test result therefore stands as a result about the delivered
regularized parametrization, and no curvature in bit-width is
identified by the development data.

\subsection{Pre-compression statistics as additional inputs}
\label{sec:app_inputs}

Three strictly separated input budgets were compared on the nine-state development panel with
leave-one-source-state-out and leave-one-size-out splits and a rule fixed in advance: the K0 state
alone; K0 with a dense loss anchor; and K0 with three dense statistics measured by one forward pass
per state and capability (the reference-token logit margin, the trace of the loss Hessian in logit
space, and the logit variance). The statistics help inside a parametric family, through ridge, in
four of nine arm-and-capability pairs (pruning math and QA, grouped-quantization math and QA), each
with an interval above zero; in nine of nine pairs both statistics-augmented linear candidates still
have higher error than the source-free median configuration curve. Accuracy at naming the most
fragile capability falls for pruning (58.3\% to 41.7\%) and rises for grouped quantization (40.7\% to
49.4\%) when the statistics are added.

One explanation was then tested and closed: that the source-dependent candidates lose because they
re-learn the whole response. Holding the median configuration curve fixed and fitting only a heavily
shrunk source correction to its residual, with the shrinkage strength selected inside training folds
from a grid fixed in advance, yields a positive gain with an interval excluding zero in zero of nine
primary pairs against a pre-fixed requirement of five; the data prefers a finite correction in most
folds, and it does not convert into held-out gain. No further statistics or richer corrections are
proposed.


\subsection{Conditioning on the initial model state}
\label{sec:app_conditioning}

On the nine-state development panel with one stage held out, a
per-setting regression of the pruning response on $(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c})$
lowers the error from 0.413, 0.516, and 0.500 nats to 0.253, 0.292,
and 0.806 for math, code, and QA; adding $L_{0,c}$ to $(N_0,D_0)$ has
intervals above zero for math and code, adding $D_0$ does not
(Fig.~\ref{fig:inputs}A). The source-conditioned forms below use the
three inputs per capability, and every arm also carries a source-free
relation for the cases where the source inputs add nothing.

\subsection{Selection and boundary of the delivered relations}
\label{sec:app_selbound}

\paragraph{Pruning: selection.} Fit on nine states at $d\in\{0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$
and scored at the unseen 0.65 and 0.55, Eq.~\ref{eq:power} cuts the
strength-only error two- to threefold and A2 matches it; refit on 17
states under a pre-committed leave-one-source-out rule, A2 (0.479)
and the power form (0.494) tie within 0.02 nats and the rule prefers
the continuous form that vanishes at $d=1$ (coefficients in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_coef}; $\gamma_c$ is a fitted constant, not
established as necessary).

\paragraph{Pruning: boundary.} Evaluated as a frozen candidate on three new checkpoints, the power form reaches A2's math error with five parameters against twenty and does not beat the median curve on code or QA; on a 2.8B source the median curve is three to seven times better than either source form (\S\ref{sec:general}). The compact form trades accuracy for compactness within a tested range; the median curve is the relation for new states, a rule fixed after these tests and reused frozen in \S\ref{sec:selection}.

\paragraph{Quantization: selection.} Two parametric candidates were tested first, a
separable amplitude-times-shape form and a low-order surface in
$\log_2 q_{\max}$ and $\log_2 g$; the surface beat zero change at the
unseen $b=4$ and $g=128$ on the two-bit-level development set, but its
quadratic term is unidentified there (rank 16 of 20,
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}). In a confirmation round with all 54
unblinded cells as development set and 21 cells frozen at unseen
group sizes and on a new state, the interpolation won the frozen cells
for math and code and the median won the new state, so the delivered
rule was fixed after that round and is labeled retrospective
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}).

\paragraph{Quantization: boundary.} The interpolation holds at unseen group sizes of
seen states; on the new state the source-free median is as good or
better except for code, so source conditioning adds nothing there,
and the surface is not delivered (\S\ref{sec:unseen_settings}).

\paragraph{Distillation: selection.} Eight forms in $T$, $E$, and a source term were
frozen on twelve development runs and scored on nine unseen-pool
trajectories with the headline chosen after the test, and two
descriptor-carrying forms were compared retrospectively
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}); the delivered forms were then
re-selected on all 25 trajectories by a pre-committed
leave-one-trajectory-out rule and frozen for six new pools of
$U{=}200$ at three new budgets. A linear form in the student state, fit on nine Pythia states, serves only as the math predictor for distillation candidates in \S\ref{sec:selection}.

\paragraph{Distillation: boundary.} At the new pool size the selected form beats the
frozen strongest baseline for code on both students, where that
baseline is the same $\log(1+E)$ form with an intercept, so the gain
supports the zero-budget boundary and parsimony of the form, not a
full student-data-training law; QA gains on 270M only, math loses to intercept baselines, no selected form is the post-hoc best of the sixteen frozen forms, a minimum selection favours by construction (Table~\ref{tab:main_final}); a 6.6-times-larger student is out of range.


\subsection{Applicability across capabilities}
\label{sec:app_applic}

Where the initial-state inputs help is method- and range-specific:
they carry math and code at unseen pruning densities of seen sizes
and at unseen group sizes of seen states, add nothing on new
quantization states or in distillation beyond the reference anchor,
and over-predict the QA pruning response on every new checkpoint. What capability conditioning adds is measurable on the frozen
confirmation panels (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}): per-capability
pruning curves beat a shared damage curve with a signed
per-capability scale by 0.06 nats macro (interval $[0.02,0.10]$ with
the 2.8B duplicate merged), almost entirely through QA (0.17,
$[0.11,0.24]$), whose aggregate curve is not proportional to the tested shared curve:
it changes sign across density (damage at $d=0.55$, improvement from
0.6 to 0.9), so no scale of that curve, including a negative one,
represents it ($R^2$ 0.49 against 0.94 and 0.97 for math and code),
and its direction also varies across sources. For math and
code a shared curve is as good or better, for grouped quantization
the two are indistinguishable (0.003 nats), and for distillation the
comparison is empty because the one-coefficient forms are
algebraically equivalent. Conditioning matters where the shape of the
response differs across capabilities.

\paragraph{Shared structure and ranges.} One function family across
methods holds for pruning by construction and fails for quantization
and distillation; within pruning one exponent shared across
capabilities costs nothing, whereas quantization coefficients must be
capability-specific; parameter intervals and 80\% prediction
intervals that under-cover (78\% and 67\%) are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}.

\subsection{Shared structure, parameter ranges, and one-point calibration}
\label{sec:app_shared}

\paragraph{Shared structure, ranges, and calibration.} One function
family for all three methods, with method- and capability-specific
parameters, holds for pruning by construction and fails for the other two arms on
the same held-out splits (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}). Within pruning one exponent shared across capabilities costs nothing
(leave-one-source MAE within 0.02 nats of per-capability exponents),
whereas the quantization coefficients must be capability-specific. Three ranges are kept apart
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}): the bootstrap intervals of the pruning coefficients exclude zero for
the dense-anchor term on math and code and not on QA; per-source
exponents spread widely (3.0 to 6.0 for math); and 80\% prediction
intervals from leave-one-source residuals
under-cover (78\% on the 27 confirmation-panel cells, 67\% on the 1B
and 6.9B pair cells) at a mean full width near 1.5 nats. One-point calibration of the pruning amplitude at the mildest density
amplifies noise and is no shortcut (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}).

\subsection{Pythia-1B and 6.9B pairs under two protocols}
\label{sec:app_p1v2}

The second frozen round (Table~\ref{tab:p1v2}) added seven pruning
densities (0.9, 0.8, 0.75, 0.7, 0.65 and 0.6 as interpolation, 0.55 as
extrapolation) and five bit-widths (8, 6, 5, 4, 3) on Pythia-1B at
steps 32k and 112k. Two fitting protocols were frozen at the same time.
Protocol A fits each candidate on the full nine-state development
panel. Protocol B fits on development states at step 64k or below, so
that its 112k rows test a training-stage extrapolation on top of the
size change. The 5-bit prediction of the class-indicator candidate is a
fixed rule, the average of its committed 4- and 6-bit predictions,
chosen so that no 5-bit measurement enters any fit. Candidate names
follow \S\ref{sec:law_prune}: power is the shared power form, A2 the
per-density source regression with a fixed interpolation rule, A1 its
$\gamma=1$ variant, cont a continuous two-term polynomial in $1-d$, and
the three source-free baselines are strength-only, the median
development curve, and zero change. For quantization, full uses
$\{N_0, L_0, D_0\}$, no-D0 drops the training-stage term, and mean and
median are per-bit constants from the development panel.

Per capability, at 112k under protocol A the source regression reaches
MAE 0.111 (math), 0.091 (code) and 0.135 (QA); the strongest
source-free alternative is strength-only for math (0.117) and the
median curve for code (0.107) and QA (0.261). At 32k the QA error of
the source regression rises to 0.339 while the median curve stays at
0.193, which accounts for most of the aggregate gap. Under protocol B at
112k the power and A2 forms are ahead of strength-only at $d=0.55$
(0.96 and 1.00 against 1.33), but with errors near one nat the ordering
carries little practical value. The pair cost 22 A100 minutes.

The 6.9B pair, a size extrapolation of about five times, was measured
under the same freeze (36 A100 minutes, reference model on CPU). Its
dense anchors are math 1.27, code 1.30 and QA 5.11 at 32k, and 1.11,
1.21 and 5.09 at 112k. Per capability under protocol A at 112k, the
source regression reaches 0.23 (math), 0.19 (code) and 0.96 (QA)
against 0.05, 0.07 and 0.15 for the median curve. Under protocol B the
source forms lead on code only (A2 0.058 and 0.083 at the two stages
against 0.208 and 0.074 for the median curve) and trail on QA (0.32 and
0.20 against 0.08 and 0.15). At $d=0.55$ the source-free curves win
under both protocols, with source-conditioned errors between 0.55 and
2.2 nats. For quantization under protocol A the class-indicator
candidate is worst or near worst above 3 bits (0.25 and 0.27 against
0.03 and 0.04 for the per-bit median) and overshoots by about four nats
at 3 bits. Protocol B compresses the differences above 3 bits to the
0.02 to 0.05 range and leaves the candidate ahead at 3 bits only for the
late stage (1.29 against 3.8 to 4.1).

\section{Capability-Loss Measurement Checks}
\label{sec:app_measure}
\paragraph{Capability specificity as a prediction test.} On a
six-model, five-density pruning panel ($d$ from $0.9$ to $0.7$), the
response on the primary benchmark of each capability was used to
predict the response on the secondary benchmark of the same capability
through a two-parameter affine map fit out of fold, and compared with
the best cross-capability predictor chosen the same way. With whole
models held out, the same-capability gain is $+0.04$ nats for math
(95\% model-bootstrap interval $[0.03,0.05]$) and $+0.14$ for QA
($[0.02,0.27]$); with whole densities held out it is $+0.14$
($[0.03,0.30]$) and $+0.21$ ($[0.02,0.52]$). For code the
cross-capability predictor is as good or better ($-0.06$,
$[-0.16,-0.00]$ with densities held out), so code specificity is not
established at this resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}). Six model clusters give descriptive
intervals only.



\paragraph{Same-capability agreement across the three operations.} On
Gemma-3-1B, six states fixed before the measurement (pruning at
$d=0.7$, RTN int4, and four distillation checkpoints of the uniform
protocol at $U=75$ and $U=450$) were scored on the main-protocol probe
and on an independent benchmark of the same capability
(Table~\ref{tab:p3_check}). Code agrees in sign and size on all six
states. Pruning and quantization damage agrees in sign on all three
capabilities, with sizes that differ on the short secondary probes
(260 and 328 scored tokens for SVAMP and TriviaQA). The QA improvement
under distillation on the primary probe ($-0.7$ to $-1.5$ nats) does
not appear on TriviaQA ($+0.1$ to $+0.4$), so the distillation QA
response reported in Section~\ref{sec:law_distill} is specific to the
probe distribution, which shares its multi-hop format with the teacher
pool, and should not be read as a general QA gain. The math response
under distillation on SVAMP lies within that probe's noise.
\input{tables/p3_check}

\paragraph{An independent multi-hop distribution.} The primary QA
probe is drawn from 2WikiMultihopQA; the teacher pool's QA traces come
from HotpotQA, a related but distinct multi-hop set. We therefore scored every trajectory
of the uniform distillation protocol at its final checkpoint, the
early P3 checkpoints, and the three dense students on 128 MuSiQue
answerable-dev questions \citep{trivedi2022musique} with the
supporting paragraphs supplied, under the same template, context
condition, and probe seed as the primary probe
(Table~\ref{tab:musique_scope}). The sign flips on every trajectory:
at moderate reuse ($E$ of 2.3 to 2.9) the primary loss falls by 0.4 to
0.9 nats while the MuSiQue loss rises by 0.1 to 0.9 nats, more for
larger students; at high reuse ($E\approx14$) both rise by several
nats; and the 1B early checkpoints already diverge ($-1.0$ to $-1.4$
against $+0.3$). The distillation QA response that the laws of
\S\ref{sec:law_distill} predict is therefore the conditional loss on
the 2Wiki distribution (the teacher pool draws its QA traces from
HotpotQA, a different Wikipedia multi-hop set), and it does not carry
over to MuSiQue even with supporting paragraphs supplied. Context
conditions differ across the sets and are part of the scope statement:
2Wiki supplies all paragraphs, MuSiQue the supporting paragraphs, and
TriviaQA none.
\input{tables/musique_scope}

\paragraph{Pre-specified scope check on eight states.} On Gemma-3-1B,
eight states fixed before measurement (dense; pruning at $d=0.85$ and
$0.7$; per-channel int4 and grouped $b=4$, $g=128$; the U450
distillation trajectory at 35k, 140k, and 280k supervised tokens) were
scored on three sets of 256 references each: a new sample of the
primary 2Wiki distribution that overlaps neither existing probe half,
MuSiQue answerable-dev with supporting paragraphs (zero overlap with
the teacher traces), and the TriviaQA control (Table~\ref{tab:qa_scope}). At
every distillation budget the pre-stated reading is ``only the new
primary sample reproduces'': the 2Wiki gain replicates ($-1.55$,
$-1.26$, $-0.88$ nats), MuSiQue and TriviaQA rise ($+0.08$ to $+0.34$).
Pruning and quantization damage agrees in sign on all three sets.
\input{tables/qa_scope}

\paragraph{Loss and behavior.} Loss-to-accuracy links were fit on
GSM8K accuracy, MBPP pass@1, and TriviaQA exact match and token F1
across seven pruned models; all four outcomes are compatible with one
cross-family sigmoid in the loss, with no resolved family-specific
term. A lower QA loss does not by itself imply better QA behavior: the
four pruned OLMo-3-32B cells whose QA loss lies below dense show no
increase in exact match. This audit covers pruning only.

\paragraph{Reference conditions.} For a subset of models the
direct-answer and given-reasoning-answer conditional distributions were
measured in addition to the full-solution reference; they differ from
it for math and QA and coincide for code, where the reference is the
function body, so the numbers in this paper are specific to the
full-solution reference and are not evidence of format robustness.

The loss is the conditional cross-entropy of the reference completion
given the prompt, summed over completion tokens and divided by the
number of completion tokens, as in Eq.~\ref{eq:taskloss}. Prompts and
references are tokenized with the model's own tokenizer and truncated to
1024 tokens; prompt tokens are masked out of the loss. All within-model
responses $\Delta L_c$ are differences in native-token nats
on identical token sequences. Because native-token units differ across
tokenizers, cross-family comparisons of absolute levels use per-byte
normalization or are reported separately, and no cross-tokenizer
quantity is treated as a common numerical scale. Negative responses are
retained as measured; nothing is clipped at zero.
