The low-order surface tested for grouped quantization is
\begin{equation}

\paragraph{Identifiability of the two-dimensional form.} The

and the ridge penalty resolves the four undetermined directions, leaving 15.9 effective degrees of freedom per capability, as Table~\ref{tab:quant_ident} records. At the unseen $b=4$, where $u\approx0$
and $u^2\approx0$, the prediction depends on how the ridge split the
fitted intercept between the two columns. A same-input design without

the same development fit, but predicts the unseen bit-width much less accurately, with bit-test errors of 1.30, 1.39 and 1.94 nats against 0.19, 0.21 and 0.44, as do
the bit-only and bit-plus-group additive designs; at $g=128$ all three
reduced designs coincide because the group terms vanish. The frozen

parametrization; the development data do not identify any curvature in bit-width without the ridge choice.

\subsection{Re-analysis of an earlier ability-space grid}
\label{sec:app_cross}

An earlier internal study fitted compression laws directly on Rasch
abilities from $\sim$100-item panels over a 774-cell Qwen3 grid and
reported that no derived law had held-out predictive power. Our
re-analysis attributes much of this outcome to the measurement layer:
first, the falsification anchor of that study was driven almost entirely by the answer-only recipe, and after stratification the anchor holds and the held-out error drops from $9.0\times$ to $1.8\times$ the noise floor; second, same-benchmark evaluation inflated the QA gains by roughly one third; third, discrimination heterogeneity invalidates interval-scale ability while rankings survive with Spearman $\rho\ge0.98$; and fourth, the item-level loss change is smooth before the cliff, which excludes discrete skill deletion at the item level.
These findings fixed the measurement protocol described in Section 2.1 of the main paper.
