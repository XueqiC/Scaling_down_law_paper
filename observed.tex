\section{Empirical Analysis of Capability Loss}
\label{sec:observed}

This section reports what the controlled measurements show: which response shapes different
capabilities share, how the initial model state and the compression configuration move those
responses, and how training data and the evaluation distribution move them under distillation.

\subsection{Compression Responses across Capabilities}
\label{sec:resp_capabilities}

Compression changes capabilities unevenly, and the order in which it changes them is not fixed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On the controlled Pythia panel, mathematics and code follow one
shared shape in density with a capability-specific scale, whereas the question-answering response
changes sign across density, as Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses} shows at every state.

The two compression axes carry different shapes. Under pruning, once the measured first-order
alignment term is subtracted, the residual before the cliff is a power law in the deleted capability
mass, with an exponent that transfers across families. Under quantization the response is instead a
threshold in bit width whose position depends on the group size, so an interpolation over the
measured grid predicts it where a smooth surface does not. Both descriptions read measurements of
the compressed model, so they explain a response rather than predict one; Appendix~\ref{sec:app_mech}
develops them, including the logit-displacement account of the loss change.

\subsection{Effects of Model State and Compression Configuration}
\label{sec:state_config}

The initial model state shapes the response, but only within a range. Parameter count and
pretraining tokens enter the pruning amplitude for mathematics and code, and neither improves the
question-answering prediction: size and stage carry the first two capabilities to unseen densities
of states that entered the fit, and over-predict question answering, whose density response is not
proportional to any shared curve. Inside the development range the source-conditioned forms and the
median density curve agree to within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B source, while far outside it
the source-conditioned forms are the less accurate of the two (\S\ref{sec:general}), and dense
pre-compression statistics are less accurate than the source-free median curve in nine of nine
pairs. Under quantization, precision and grouping act together: the threshold in bit width moves
with the group size, so a configuration is not summarised by its bit width alone.

\subsection{Effects of Training Data and Evaluation Distribution}
\label{sec:why}

Distillation adds a dependence on the training protocol that is as strong as the dependence on the
method itself. With every other setting held fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss
increase on mathematics and code grows by an order of magnitude between learning rates of
$5\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$. At the lowest of these rates the 1B student is almost unharmed on those two capabilities, and it still gains $0.79$ nats on question answering, the largest gain in the pilot.

Once the learning rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates, and question answering bears
most of the cost. At a
matched budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a pool reused about
twelve times, a reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$ near twelve, costs question answering $+0.29$, $+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats on
students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters, and nine times more independent data
converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each student. Across the same students and budget, two pool seeds per cell place the noise from the pool draw an
order of magnitude below this reuse cost on every capability.

The reuse cost transfers across question-answering distributions; the gain does not. Rescored on a
fresh 2Wiki sample disjoint from the probe, on MuSiQue and on TriviaQA, all three distributions
worsen at high reuse and improve as reuse falls, on each of the three students at the matched
budget of Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}. Only the fresh 2Wiki sample crosses into a gain there:
MuSiQue flattens near zero and TriviaQA remains positive at every reuse level we scored.

Two structural checks bound how this response may be written. A joint form whose pool term and
budget term enter through separate logarithms implies that the pool-change effect, divided by its
logarithmic bracket, is flat in budget; for code on the 1B student it drifts by a factor of twenty
across checkpoints (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:drift}). To ask whether additivity itself fails we
replayed the trainer's update boundaries to place four trajectories on a rectangle to the token; the
second difference across it, zero for any additive structure, was $-0.24$ nats on the 1B student and
$-0.21$ on the 4B for the question-answering probe, inside the pre-registered band of $\pm0.32$ and
between the additive prediction of zero and the $-0.49$ of the frozen interaction candidate
(Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:corner_test}). The registered rule therefore neither rejects nor establishes
additivity, and we deliver the reuse term in its logarithmic form.

These responses can be turned into an empirical data requirement. A registered experiment of
eighteen trajectories on the 1B and 4B students, with pools from nine thousand supervised tokens to
the full pool of 158 thousand and three evaluation budgets, measured the range of independent data
at which a loss constraint is met on the fresh 2Wiki sample, on MuSiQue and on TriviaQA
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dreq}). On the fresh 2Wiki sample the constraint is crossed inside the tested pools
for both students at the two larger budgets. On MuSiQue the largest pool still fails at tight tolerances, and a crossing appears only
at looser ones. On TriviaQA the largest pool fails for the 4B student at every tolerance, so the
requirement is a lower bound. A low-degree boundary relation did not predict these ranges more accurately than a per-student
monotone interpolation. We therefore report the requirement as measured intervals, which hold for the data source, the
maximum pool size and the distillation protocol used in these experiments.

These observations motivate predictive relations that account for the compression configuration
while allowing responses to differ across capabilities.
