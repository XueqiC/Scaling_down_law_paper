\section{What Compression Does to Capabilities}
\label{sec:observed}

The measurements show first that damage is uneven across capabilities and that the ordering is
not fixed. Across twelve public models the same pruning
density leaves QA the least damaged capability in some series and the most damaged in others
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heterogeneity}). On the controlled Pythia panel, math and code respond to density along one shared shape with a
capability-specific scale, while the QA response changes sign across density
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pythia_responses}).

The source state shapes this response inside a range: parameter count and pretraining tokens both
enter the pruning amplitude for math and code, and neither improves the QA prediction.
Inside the development range the source-conditioned forms, a per-density regression and the median
density curve agree within 0.05 nats at unseen stages of a 1B source; at a 6.9B source, five times
the development range, the source-conditioned forms lose to the median curve on math (0.30 against
0.12 nats) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code, as the tests of \S\ref{sec:general} show.

Distillation adds a dependence on the protocol that is as strong as the dependence on the method.
With all settings except the learning rate held fixed (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:lr_pilot}), the loss
increase on math and code grows with the rate: at $5\times10^{-5}$ the 1B student is almost unharmed ($+0.003$ and $+0.007$ nats) and
still gains $0.79$ nats on QA, and at $1\times10^{-4}$ the increase is an order of magnitude larger
($+0.051$ and $+0.052$). At $1\times10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$ the 4B student degrades about
twice as much as the 1B on math, and at the lowest rate the 4B is harmed ($+0.058$) where the 1B is
not.

Once the rate is fixed, the size of the data pool dominates: reusing a small pool is costly, the
cost rises with student size, and QA bears most of it. At one rate and a matched
budget $T$ near 200k supervised tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses_v2}), a pool of $D_U$ supervised
tokens reused about twelve times (reuse ratio $E=T/D_U$) costs QA $+0.29$, $+2.55$ and $+4.85$ nats
on students of 100M, 700M and 3.2B non-embedding parameters, and nine times more independent data
converts the same budget into a gain near one nat on each student; math and code damage also falls
monotonically with reuse. Two pool seeds per cell put the pool-draw noise near 0.02 nats on code and
0.13 nats on QA, small against the reuse cost.

The reuse cost also transfers across QA distributions, whereas the gain does not. Scored on a fresh
2Wiki sample, MuSiQue and TriviaQA, all three distributions worsen at high reuse and improve
monotonically as reuse falls, for each student. Only 2Wiki crosses into a gain; MuSiQue flattens near zero and TriviaQA stays positive at each
reuse level, and the 4B student is damaged about twice as much as the 1B on all three distributions.

Together these observations fix what a law must carry: a capability-specific shape under pruning,
a configuration-dependent threshold under quantization, and a distillation reuse cost that is
monotone in $E=T/D_U$, grows with student size and separates by evaluation distribution in sign.
Section~\ref{sec:why} asks what accounts for these features and \S\ref{sec:laws} builds on them.
