\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads need specific capabilities, and the models that serve them are usually
reference models scaled down by pruning, quantization, or distillation. We study, systematically and
with one validation standard across the three methods, under which inputs, compression configurations
and evaluation scopes a parsimonious relation predicts the loss conditioned on a capability and an
evaluation distribution, and which plausible structures fail under independent test. Measurements on
a controlled panel of Pythia checkpoints, whose size and pretraining stage vary independently, and on a
Gemma distillation panel with a fixed protocol establish what any law must reproduce: damage that is
uneven across capabilities with an ordering that changes across models; a source-state dependence
that holds inside a range and not beyond it; distillation damage set by the learning rate; and a cost
of reusing a small data pool that is monotone in the reuse ratio, grows with student size, reproduces
across three question-answering distributions, and reverses sign only on one. The relations that
survive frozen tests are method-specific and bounded: a five-parameter pruning form for math and code
inside its density range, a same-input interpolation over the measured quantization grid, and
one-coefficient reuse forms for distillation on the development students. Structures that did not
survive are reported with what excluded them: a logarithmic reuse term fails its own flatness
implication, dense source statistics lose to a source-free configuration curve in nine of nine
cases, and a fit-free rectangle test on two students neither rejects nor establishes additivity of
budget and reuse. A second-order account of compression damage explains the measured losses across
three families inside a magnitude boundary without predicting them. We connect the relations to a
capability- and budget-specific selection problem validated prospectively on four fresh states, state
the calibration cost and range of every prediction, and release coefficients, registered
predictions, measurements and code. The public code repository is
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/scaling_down_law}.
\end{abstract}
