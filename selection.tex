\section{Compression Method Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

A relation earns its place by changing a decision. This section turns the relations of
Section~\ref{sec:laws} into a choice among compression configurations under a storage budget and tests
that choice frozen, on references that supplied it no outcome.

\subsection{Selection under Storage Constraints}
\label{sec:selection_rule}

Given a reference model, a capability and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ whose nominal storage, stored matrix
parameters rather than runtime memory or sparse indexing, falls within $B$. A workload needing
several capabilities at once replaces $\widehat L_{m,c}$ by the largest loss increase
$\max_c[\widehat L_c(M)-L_c(M_0)]$. On the controlled panel,
the law-based choice selects the method of the measured best candidate in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289
cells for mathematics, code and question answering. We then locked a complete rule and froze its
maps for four fresh Pythia states, where they come within 0.14 nats of the best candidate and below
quantization alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main}). Its distillation candidates were historical, so a
second confirmation used candidates trained in the same round.

Each of the four references received twelve candidates at seventeen storage budgets: four pruning densities, six quantization settings, the
dense model and one student distilled in this round. Two are Pythia checkpoints at a stage with no
earlier recorded use; the two Gemma-3 models carry weights measured under other interventions, so
for them the round tests new candidates on familiar weights (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:s3-identity}). Every predicted loss and both selection maps were sealed before any
outcome was observed. Define the opportunity at a budget as the loss of the best feasible quantization candidate minus
that of the best feasible candidate of any method, and the regret of a policy as the loss of its
own choice minus that same candidate. Question answering carries an opportunity of 0.12 to 0.89 nats. Over all 68 question-answering
cells, four references at seventeen budgets each, the measured best candidate is the new student in
59; of the nine remaining cells, three admit no feasible candidate at any method, five prefer
pruning and one quantization. The two policies are compared on the 64 cells, sixteen budgets per
reference, at which both find a feasible candidate, and the rule selects the new student in 63 of
those. Its regret there is 0.000 nats on three references and 0.027 on the fourth, against 0.41 to
1.84 nats for a policy restricted to quantization, which also errs inside that family. For mathematics, code and the largest loss increase, the opportunity is at most 0.047 nats.
Quantization already supplies a near-best candidate there, the rule matches it, and in three of the
256 paired cells, those same sixteen budgets across four references and four objectives, all at the
budget that admits the dense model, the rule exceeds its regret by at most 0.0015 nats. All four references meet the criterion registered for this round.

To separate the contribution of distillation from the value of a smaller model, we retrospectively add the pristine student as a control at the same nominal storage cost. Replacing the pristine student with its distilled counterpart, with quantization available in both candidate sets, increases the question-answering opportunity by 0.084 to 0.650 nats across the four references. The corresponding opportunity changes for math and code are non-positive (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3-retrospective-control}). These are changes in the opportunity open to the candidate set, not loss changes of a student
against its own starting point: the control marks where distillation creates an opening for
selection and where quantization already supplies a competitive candidate.
