\section{Independent Tests: Configurations, Model States, and Capabilities}
\label{sec:general}

Every test below scores predictions frozen before measurement. Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}
gives the error of each relation against its development-stage baseline with its status;
Fig.~\ref{fig:generalization} shows the same comparison per row; per-cell results are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}.



\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

On three Pythia checkpoints that entered no fit, the frozen pruning power form matches the
per-density regression on math with a quarter of the parameters (0.24 against 0.23 nats), trails the
median on code, and loses QA to every source-free curve; at $d=0.55$, outside the range, every
source-conditioned form fails by 0.6 to 4.5 nats. In grouped quantization, with predictions frozen on all 54 cells, unseen group sizes on the
development states are predicted best by the piecewise interpolation for math and code (0.07 and
0.12 nats), and on a state absent from the fit by the median for math and QA and the interpolation
for code. For distillation, at six
new pools and three new budgets every selected form beats zero change and the gain over the
development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students (0.004 and 0.005 nats) and for
QA on the 270M only (Table~\ref{tab:main-prediction-v2}).

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

Inside the range the frozen forms and the median curve lie within 0.05 nats of one another at
unseen stages of a 1B source. Outside it, at a 6.9B source
five times the development range, the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on
math (0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code, the per-bit quantization
regression is worse than the per-bit median at every bit-width, and on a 2.8B source inside the size
range the frozen median density curve predicts within 0.08, 0.05 and 0.07 nats while both
source-conditioned forms are three to seven times worse (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}). For
distillation the 4B student, 6.6 times the reference size and a development student in the final
round, keeps only its QA gain at the development pools (0.83, $[0.12,1.54]$) and is beaten by the
frozen constant at the unseen pool on every capability (Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}).

\paragraph{The range each relation has earned.} Figure~\ref{fig:generalization} separates the kinds
of novelty. New configurations of seen states are where the initial-state inputs pay: unseen pruning
densities inside the range and unseen group sizes on development states are predicted within their
stated error. New sources and students are where they do not: at five times the development size
the source-conditioned pruning forms lose to a median curve, and the 4B distillation student keeps
only its QA gain. New evaluation distributions split by sign. A relation from this paper should be used
inside the panel it was tested on, with a one-point calibration when the state is new; beyond that
its status is a recommendation, not a prediction.
