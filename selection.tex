\section{From Laws to Capability-Specific Selection}
\label{sec:selection}


The relations turn configuration choice into a computation: given a reference model, a capability
and a budget, the decision is
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ holds the settings of method $m$ within
budget $B$. Retrospectively, on the controlled panel the law-based choice selects the method of the oracle
in 96\%, 95\% and 73\% of 289 cells for math, code and QA. A complete rule was then locked, the predicted loss of each candidate and the four maps were
frozen for four fresh Pythia states with 18 configurations each, and only then measured (Fig.~\ref{fig:rule_main_v2}). Over 68 cells per objective the frozen-rule maps are within 0.00001, 0.004 and 0.14 nats of the
oracle for math, code and QA, below the source-conditioned maps and below quantization-only under the same candidates, meeting
the registered criterion; the rule trails quantization-only on one state. The math and code margins come largely from the one state with historical distillation
candidates; elsewhere the two coincide.


The prospective result establishes the rule on four Pythia states under nominal storage budgets;
validation across model families with fresh distillation candidates is the natural next step.
