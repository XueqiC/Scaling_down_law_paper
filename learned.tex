\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:learned}

This study investigates capability scaling-down laws for LLM compression through controlled measurements, predictive relations, and independent validation. Compact pruning predictors reduce development configuration measurements within a controlled domain, while quantization benefits from configuration-based interpolation and medians. Distillation exhibits reuse penalties that vary with student size and evaluation distribution, motivating empirical data-requirement intervals and bounds. Prospective selection tests and a pristine-student control identify additional QA opportunities under nominal storage constraints. Together, these findings connect predictive accuracy, measurement requirements, and decision value, and clarify where capability-conditioned prediction is useful.

Three boundaries fix where these results apply. Measurement efficiency and stable transfer are
different properties: the compact pruning form reaches the full-budget regression from half the
development measurements inside the controlled domain, and does not thereby carry to states far
outside it. The configuration range, the student state and the evaluation distribution bound each
relation, which is why the distillation requirement is reported as measured intervals on the
distributions we scored. The selection evidence holds for the candidate sets and the nominal
storage budgets tested, and what it returns depends on the capability: where quantization already
supplies a near-best candidate the cross-method choice has little to add, which is itself part of
the answer.
