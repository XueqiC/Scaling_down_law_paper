\section{Why the Responses Vary: Conditions and Mechanism}
\label{sec:why}

This section asks which conditions and mechanisms account for the variation in
\S\ref{sec:observed}. The answers fall into verified causes, excluded explanations and open factors.




\subsection{Function shape}
\label{sec:why_shape}

Under pruning the pre-cliff response is a power law in the deleted capability mass with a shared
exponent that transfers across families; under quantization it is a
bit-width threshold whose position depends on the group size, so an interpolation over the
measured grid predicts the response and a smooth surface does not.

Under distillation, a joint form with a logarithmic reuse term,
$\delta=(a+a'z)\log(1+T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})+(b+b'z)\log(1+E)$, implies that the pool-change effect
divided by its log bracket is flat in budget; it drifts by a factor of twenty across checkpoints for
code on the 1B student at matching tolerances of 1\% and 0.3\%. A free reuse exponent $h_p(E)=((1+E)^p-1)/p$, profiled inside training folds, lies near or above
one for each capability (code 0.69, math 1.12, QA 1.44; Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}a) with wide
fold ranges and yields no held-out accuracy: neither the logarithmic form, the curved form nor a
single interaction term beats the development-stage strongest baseline on either pre-declared
target.

Whether additivity itself failed cannot be determined from those data, because a fixed pool and a
doubling budget grid do not place two trajectories on the same budget. Four trajectories designed by
replaying the update boundaries of the trainer landed on a rectangle to the token. The second
difference across it, zero for any additive structure regardless of its curvature,
was $-0.24$ nats on the 1B student and $-0.21$ on the 4B for the QA probe, inside the pre-registered
band of $\pm0.32$ and between the additive prediction of zero and the $-0.49$ of the frozen interaction
candidate (Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}b). The pre-fixed rule did not reject additivity and does not
establish it; the 4B statistic on math and code fell outside its band at readouts that were registered as
underpowered.

\subsection{Source state, protocol, and evaluation distribution}
\label{sec:why_conditions}

Size and stage carry math and code to unseen densities of seen states and over-predict QA, whose
density response is not proportional to any shared curve. Richer descriptors did not improve transfer: dense pre-compression statistics improve a
parametric family in four of nine pairs and lose to the source-free median configuration curve in
nine of nine, with or without a shrunk source correction. Under distillation
the learning rate sets the damage magnitude (\S\ref{sec:observed}); at a fixed rate the reuse ratio
$E=T/D_U$ sets a QA cost that is monotone, grows with student size, and transfers across the three
QA distributions while the gain is confined to 2Wiki. A data requirement phrased as a bound on the loss increase is supportable in form across
distributions, whereas a claim that distillation improves question answering holds only for 2Wiki.

\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:why_mech}

For pruning and quantization the loss change under a logit displacement $r$ obeys the exact identity
$\Delta L=(\mathbb{E}_p[r]-r_y)+\tfrac12\mathrm{Var}_p(r)+O(\lVert r\rVert^3)$, and its
coefficient-free second-order truncation reproduces the measured damage across three families with
one boundary set by damage magnitude, under 5\% median relative error below about 0.1 nats and past
20\% by 0.25 (Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}c). Pure logit shrinkage carries about three percent of the response; the residual variance that
carries the rest needs the compressed model, so the account explains the damage after the fact and
enters no relation as an input.

\subsection{What is settled, excluded, and open}
\label{sec:why_summary}

\emph{Verified causes.} The learning rate sets distillation damage magnitude; the reuse ratio sets
the QA cost, monotonically and with student size; the response shape differs across capabilities
under pruning, where capability conditioning gains 0.06 nats macro-averaged (0.17 on QA); compression damage
is second-order in logit displacement inside a magnitude boundary.

\emph{Excluded explanations.} A logarithmic reuse term with budget-independent coefficients. Pure
logit shrinkage as the damage mechanism. Source descriptors as a general route to cross-source
transfer within the inputs tested.

\emph{Unidentified.} Whether budget and reuse interact: the QA test neither rejected nor established
additivity, and neither structure predicted the measured second difference within the registered
noise. Why the 4B second differences on math and code exceed both predictions while the 1B ones do
not. The form of the reuse term beyond the finding that it is closer to linear than logarithmic. What
predicts the data-change intervention; none of the parsimonious forms tried, and no source
descriptor, predicts it.
